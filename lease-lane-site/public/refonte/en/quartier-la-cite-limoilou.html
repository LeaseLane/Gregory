    Borough profile

    Renting an apartment in La Cité-Limoilou.

    La Cité-Limoilou is the smallest of Québec City’s six boroughs, at 22.18 km². The Saint-Charles River separates La Cité, to the south, from Limoilou, to the north. A cliff marks the boundary between Upper Town, more than 100 m above sea level, and Lower Town. The borough has nine neighbourhoods: Vieux-Québec–Cap-Blanc–colline Parlementaire, Saint-Roch, Saint-Jean-Baptiste, Montcalm, Saint-Sauveur, Saint-Sacrement, Lairet, Maizerets and Vieux-Limoilou.

    In 2021, the borough had 108,415 residents. With about 4,864 residents per km², it is the densest area of the city. Old Québec, where Champlain founded Québec City in 1608, has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985, and the Plains of Abraham, the Domaine de Maizerets and the Saint-Charles River linear park are the borough’s main green spaces.

    • Housing

      Mostly plexes and row houses, often served by back lanes in Limoilou. In Saint-Roch, former industrial buildings were converted into condominiums in the 2000s.

    • Nearby

      Plaines d’Abraham · Domaine de Maizerets · Parc linéaire de la rivière Saint-Charles

    • Good to know

      Tenants are the majority in several neighbourhoods: in 2016, about 76% of residents of Saint-Roch and Vieux-Limoilou rented their home. Public transit is provided by the Réseau de transport de la Capitale.

    History

    The history of La Cité-Limoilou.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1608

      Founding of Québec City by Champlain

    2. 1792

      The faubourgs are integrated into the city

    3. 1909

      Annexation of Limoilou to Québec City

    4. 1985

      Old Québec inscribed on the UNESCO list

    5. 2009

      Creation of the current borough

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1535 – 1608

      //

      Jacques Cartier discovered the site in 1535. It was then the site of Stadacona, an Iroquoian village. Samuel de Champlain founded Québec City in 1608, in present-day Old Québec, and the city first developed within its fortifications, at the top and the foot of Cap Diamant.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1780 – 1866

      //

      In the mid-18th century, a first faubourg, the future Saint-Roch, housed artisans and workers outside the walls. The Faubourg Saint-Jean was urbanized starting in the 1780s, and both were integrated into the city in 1792. In the 19th century, fires, including those of 1845 and 1866, levelled faubourgs that were then rebuilt.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1893 – 1909

      //

      Limoilou remained rural until the end of the 19th century. In 1893, the working-class villages of Stadacona, Hedleyville and New Waterford united under the name Limoilou, a variant of the name of Jacques Cartier’s manor in Saint-Malo. Annexed to Québec City in 1909, the area was urbanized on a grid plan, with its numbered streets and back lanes.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1960 – 2009

      //

      Until the 1960s, Saint-Roch was Québec City’s commercial downtown, with the department stores of Rue Saint-Joseph. The rise of the suburbs then led to major projects, such as Autoroute Dufferin-Montmorency. The La Cité and Limoilou boroughs, created on January 1, 2002, were merged on November 1, 2009.

    01What neighbourhoods are part of La Cité-Limoilou?

    The borough has nine neighbourhoods: Vieux-Québec–Cap-Blanc–colline Parlementaire, Saint-Roch, Saint-Jean-Baptiste, Montcalm, Saint-Sauveur, Saint-Sacrement, Lairet, Maizerets and Vieux-Limoilou.

    02Is Old Québec in La Cité-Limoilou?

    Yes. Old Québec, at the top and the foot of Cap Diamant, is part of the Vieux-Québec–Cap-Blanc–colline Parlementaire neighbourhood, which is the downtown and historic centre of the borough.

    03Where does the name Limoilou come from?

    The name is a variant of Limoëlou, Jacques Cartier’s manor in Saint-Malo, Brittany. The working-class villages north of the Saint-Charles River adopted it in 1893.
