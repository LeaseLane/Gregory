    Repossession and eviction: rules, deadlines and moratorium

    Who can repossess a unit, notice deadlines, the tenant’s response, protection for seniors and the eviction moratorium until 2027.

    01The short answer

    In Québec, a landlord can repossess a unit for themselves or a relative by giving written notice, generally 6 months before the lease ends[1]. The tenant has one month to respond and silence counts as refusal; the landlord must then apply to the TAL within the month[1][3]. Evictions to enlarge, subdivide or change the use of a dwelling are suspended until June 6, 2027[2].

    02Who can repossess a unit

    The landlord can repossess the dwelling to live in it, or to house their first-degree ascendants or descendants, or another relative or relation by marriage of whom they are the main support. They can also repossess it for a former spouse of whom they remain the main support[1].

    An undivided co-owner cannot repossess a unit, unless the other co-owner is their spouse and they are the only two owners[1].

    03Notice deadlines

    Repossession notice deadlines by lease term

    Lease termNotice to give
    More than 6 monthsAt least 6 months before the lease ends
    6 months or lessAt least 1 month before the lease ends
    Indeterminate termAt least 6 months before the date of repossession

    The notice states the date of repossession, the beneficiary’s name and their relationship to the landlord. It must also reproduce the text of article 1959.1 of the Civil Code, which protects certain seniors[1]. An incomplete or late notice exposes the repossession to refusal by the tribunal.

    04The tenant’s response and the application to the TAL

    The tenant has one month after receiving the notice to respond. If they do not respond, they are deemed to have refused to leave the unit[1].

    If the tenant refuses, it is up to the landlord to file an application with the TAL within the month that follows. They must show that they genuinely intend to repossess the unit for the stated purpose and that it is not a pretext[1][3].

    05Protection for tenants aged 65 and over

    The landlord can neither repossess nor evict when the tenant, or their spouse, meets all three of the following conditions[1][2]:

    • they are 65 or older;
    • they have occupied the unit for at least 10 years;
    • their income is equal to or less than 125% of the maximum income for eligibility for low-rent housing.

    Exceptions exist for repossession, notably when the landlord or the beneficiary is themselves 65 or older[1]. The income thresholds are published by the Société d’habitation du Québec.

    06Eviction and the moratorium until 2027

    Eviction covers another purpose: subdividing the unit, substantially enlarging it or changing its use. Bill 65, assented to on June 6, 2024, prohibits these evictions until June 6, 2027[2]. Repossession for oneself or a relative is not covered by this moratorium.

    The moratorium can end earlier if the vacancy rate published by CMHC reaches 3%, after a notice published in the Gazette officielle; the government can also exempt territories from the moratorium[2]. Check the status of the moratorium with the TAL before sending a notice.

    Comparison of repossession and eviction in Québec

    RepossessionEviction
    PurposeHousing the landlord or a relativeSubdividing, enlarging or changing the use
    Covered by the moratoriumNoYes, until June 6, 2027
    CompensationMoving costs, if the TAL orders itMoving costs and 1 month’s rent per year of occupancy (3 to 24 months)
    Protection for those 65 and overYes, with exceptionsYes

    Eviction compensation includes reasonable moving costs and one month’s rent per year of uninterrupted tenancy, with a minimum of 3 months and a maximum of 24 months[1].

    07After repossession

    A unit that has been repossessed or vacated cannot be re-rented or used for another purpose without the TAL’s authorization[1]. If the landlord does not act in good faith, the tenant can claim damages, and punitive damages in case of bad faith[1].

    Sample timeline for a lease from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027: the notice must be received no later than December 31, 2026; the tenant has one month to respond; if they refuse or do not respond, the application to the TAL is filed within the following month.

    08Frequently asked questions

    01The tenant does not respond to the notice: does that mean they accept?

    No. Silence counts as refusal (s. 1962 CCQ). The landlord must apply to the TAL within the month.

    02Does the moratorium prevent repossession for my daughter?

    No. It covers only evictions for subdivision, enlargement or change of use. Repossession remains possible, subject to the protection of seniors.

    03How much does an eviction cost?

    Reasonable moving costs, plus one month’s rent per year of occupancy, with a minimum of 3 months and a maximum of 24 months (s. 1965 CCQ).

    04Can I re-rent the unit after repossession?

    Not without the TAL’s authorization (s. 1970 CCQ).
