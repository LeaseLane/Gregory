    Town profile

    Rent an apartment in Saint-Isidore.

    Saint-Isidore occupies the northwest tip of the La Nouvelle-Beauce MRC, between the Chaudière and Etchemin rivers. It touches Saint-Henri to the north, Saint-Lambert-de-Lauzon to the west and Saint-Anselme to the east. The Chaudière River forms its western boundary, and the land rises gently to the southeast, from about 110 to 190 metres in elevation.

    The territory covers 102.66 km², mostly farmland, where pigs and dairy cows are among the animals raised. An arena built in 1978, which also houses halls and the municipal offices, is the centre of recreation. The Monk bike path, completed in 2022, follows the former Monk subdivision railway right-of-way for 4 km.

    • Housing

      Mostly single-family houses, with a few plexes and small buildings near the village. The municipality also has a residential development area.

    • Nearby

      Aréna et parc de l’aréna · Bibliothèque Laurette-Nadeau-Parent · Parc Brochu-Châtigny

    • Good to know

      Autoroute Robert-Cliche (A-73) passes through the west of the municipality, 5 km from the village. Lévis is about fifteen kilometres to the north.

    History

    The history of Saint-Isidore.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1789

      Nicolas Hamann clears the first farm

    2. 1829

      Canonical erection of the parish

    3. 1853

      Construction of the church begins

    4. 1921

      The village separates from the parish

    5. 1993

      Merger of the village and the parish

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1636 – 1789

      //

      The territory was first part of the seigneury of Lauzon, granted in 1636. The route Justinienne, which links the seigneuries of Lauzon and Joliet, opened this corner of forest to settlement. In 1789, Nicolas Hamann and his family settled alongside this road and cleared the first farm.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1801 – 1829

      //

      Real settlement began around 1801, with families from Île d’Orléans, the Côte-de-Beaupré and the seigneuries of Lauzon and Bellechasse. In 1829, Bishop Bernard-Claude Panet canonically established the parish of Saint-Isidore-de-Lauzon. The name honours Saint Isidore of Seville, a Spanish bishop who died in 636.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1853 – 1957

      //

      The present church was built from 1853 to 1856. It reuses the plan and interior décor of the Saint-Anselme church, designed by Thomas Baillairgé, and has been classified as a heritage building since 1957. The parish municipality was created in 1855.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1921 – 1993

      //

      In 1921, the village separated from the parish. A small Quebec Central station, called Le Bras, operated from 1921 to 1967. The place was once nicknamed the Gateway to the Beauce, because you entered the Beauce at Saint-Isidore coming from Lévis or Québec City. The village and the parish were reunited in 1993 to form the present municipality.

    01Where is Saint-Isidore?

    On the South Shore, in the La Nouvelle-Beauce MRC in Chaudière-Appalaches, about fifteen kilometres south of Lévis. The municipality extends east of the Chaudière River.

    02Where does the name Saint-Isidore come from?

    It honours Saint Isidore of Seville, a Spanish bishop who died in 636, and not Isidore the Farmer. The parish was first called Saint-Isidore-de-Lauzon, after the seigneury of Lauzon.

    03How do I get to Saint-Isidore from Québec City?

    By Autoroute Robert-Cliche (A-73), which passes through the west of the municipality, 5 km from the village. Routes 173 (Route du Président-Kennedy) and 275 (Route Coulombe) also cross the territory.
