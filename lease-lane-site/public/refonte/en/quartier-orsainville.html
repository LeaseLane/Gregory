    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Orsainville.

    Orsainville is bounded by Boulevard Jean-Talon to the south, the Laurentienne highway (73) to the west, Boulevard du Loiret to the east and Rue de la Faune to the north. Boulevard Henri-Bourassa runs through its centre and concentrates the density, while the rest of the territory is mostly single-family homes. The terrain is fairly flat, except in the north, where the “côte du Zoo” is located.

    At the 2021 census, the neighbourhood had 16,575 residents, and 30.7% of households were tenants. The Golf de la Faune and Parc des Moulins occupy much of the west, and the du Berger, des Commissaires and des Roches rivers run through it. It also has the Rivière-des-Roches linear park, Parcs Saint-Pierre, Chabot and des Verveines, and the Église Saint-Pierre-aux-Liens.

    • Housing

      Detached houses and apartment buildings of fewer than five storeys; the Boulevard Henri-Bourassa corridor is the densest.

    • Nearby

      Trait-Carré de Charlesbourg · Golf de la Faune · Notre-Dame-des-Laurentides

    • Good to know

      The Laurentienne highway (73) runs along the west of the neighbourhood, and Boulevard Henri-Bourassa leads toward the south of Charlesbourg.

    History

    The history of Orsainville.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1696

      Opening of the rang Saint-Pierre

    2. 1931

      Opening of the Jardin zoologique du Québec

    3. 1953

      The municipality becomes Orsainville

    4. 1976

      Merger with the Town of Charlesbourg

    5. 2006

      Creation of Parc des Moulins

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1696

      //

      Around 1696, the Chemin de Charlesbourg, today’s 1re Avenue, was extended north as the rang Saint-Pierre, now Boulevard Henri-Bourassa. The rang links the Trait-Carré de Charlesbourg to the future neighbourhood and separates two cleared areas: Saint-Pierre, to the west, and Saint-Claude, to the east. The Élie and Joubert families were the first to settle there.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1855 – 1952

      //

      From 1855, the territory was part of the vast municipality of Saint-Charles-de-Charlesbourg, split up several times until 1952. In 1931, the Jardin zoologique du Québec opened its doors on the banks of the Rivière du Berger. After the river flooded in 1938, the site was redeveloped and new buildings were built there.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1952 – 1976

      //

      In 1952, population growth led to the establishment of the Saint-Pierre-aux-Liens parish, whose church dates from 1954. In 1953, the municipality was renamed Orsainville, after the county of Orsainville, a fief from the days of New France. Having become a city in 1960, Orsainville quickly went from farming parish to suburb, then was merged with Charlesbourg in 1976.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 2002 – 2022

      //

      In 2002, Charlesbourg was merged with Québec City, and Orsainville became a neighbourhood of the borough. The Jardin zoologique closed on March 31, 2006, and Parc des Moulins was created that same year on part of its site. On October 17, 2022, the neighbourhood, until then provisionally called “quartier 4-2,” received a neighbourhood council.
