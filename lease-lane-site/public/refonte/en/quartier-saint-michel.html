    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Saint-Michel.

    Saint-Michel occupies the part of Beauport north of its historic centre. It is bounded on the west by the Beauport River and the Charlesbourg borough, on the south by Autoroute Félix-Leclerc, on the north by Boulevard Louis-XIV and on the east by the former limestone quarries. Rue Seigneuriale, Boulevard Raymond and Avenue Saint-Michel are its main arteries; big-box stores and a 16-screen cinema line the highway.

    At the 2016 census, 13.2% of residents were tenants, and the neighbourhood had 17,885 residents, 29.8% of whom were under 25. A few farms remain active on Avenue Joseph-Giffard and Boulevard Louis-XIV. It is also home to the Club de golf Beauport and the parks la Ribambelle, Chevalier, Saint-Joseph, Armand-Paris and Montpellier.

    • Housing

      Mostly single-family homes, often recent; apartments in small buildings are less common.

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1634 – 1670

      //

      The Saint-Michel territory is part of the seigneury of Beauport, granted on January 15, 1634 to Robert Giffard, a surgeon and apothecary from Mortagne-au-Perche. During the first decades, settlement was concentrated near the river, around the bourg du Fargy. In the 1670s, the second seigneur, Joseph Giffard, granted inland lands along new rangs, including the Saint-Michel and Saint-Joseph rangs.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1725 – 1860

      //

      Rue Seigneuriale is the old road that linked these rangs to the heart of the seigneury. In 1725, Beauport had about a hundred families spread over its various rangs. Avenues Saint-Michel and Joseph-Giffard, the former Rang Saint-Joseph, preserve the trace of these roads. The house at 94 Avenue Saint-Michel, built between 1830 and 1860, is among the oldest in the area.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1960 – 1976

      //

      Despite its proximity to Québec City, the area remained agricultural until the mid-20th century. To the east, huge limestone quarries supplied the Ciments Saint-Laurent cement plant; they are now disused. During the 1960s and 1970s, Beauport gradually turned into a suburb. On January 1, 1976, six neighbouring municipalities were merged into the city of Beauport.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1996 – 2016

      //

      During the municipal reorganizations of 2002, Beauport was merged with Québec City, and Saint-Michel became one of the borough’s five neighbourhoods, at first called quartier 5-2. Since the turn of the millennium, the former Rang Saint-Joseph has become a residential area, and a commercial mega-centre has set up there. Between 1996 and 2016, the neighbourhood’s population rose from 13,645 to 17,885, an increase of 31.1%.
