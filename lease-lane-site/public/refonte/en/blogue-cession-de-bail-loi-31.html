    Lease assignment: what changes with Bill 31

    The assignment notice, the 15-day period, the owner’s three possible responses and the fees allowed, since February 21, 2024.

    01The short answer

    Since February 21, 2024 (Bill 31), a landlord who receives an assignment notice has 15 days to respond[1][2]. The landlord can accept, refuse for a serious reason (the lease continues) or refuse for another reason: the lease is then terminated on the assignment date stated in the notice[1][2]. With no response within 15 days, the landlord is deemed to have accepted[1].

    02Assignment or sublet: the difference

    An assignment transfers the lease to another person, the assignee, who becomes the tenant in your place. It releases the former tenant from their obligations; in a dwelling lease, it is impossible to agree otherwise[1][2].

    A sublet allows another person to occupy the unit, but you remain the tenant and remain responsible to the landlord. The new option to refuse with termination does not apply to a sublet[2].

    03The assignment notice

    The tenant must notify the landlord in writing and give the name and address of the person to whom they want to assign the lease. Since Bill 31, the notice must also state the planned assignment date[1]. The TAL offers a notice form[4].

    Send the notice in a way that lets you prove it was received: the landlord’s response period starts on receipt, not on sending.

    04The 15-day period and the three possible responses

    The landlord’s possible responses to a lease assignment notice and their effects

    Landlord’s responseEffect
    Accepts, or does not respond within 15 daysThe assignment takes place; the assignee becomes the tenant on the planned date
    Refuses for a serious reason, in writing with reasonsThe lease continues; the tenant can contest the refusal before the TAL
    Refuses for another reasonThe lease is terminated on the assignment date stated in the notice

    The serious reason generally concerns the assignee, for example their ability to pay the rent. The landlord must give their reasons within 15 days, otherwise they are deemed to have consented[1]. The TAL publishes a response form for notices sent since February 21, 2024[3].

    05Fees and money: what is prohibited

    • A landlord who consents can only require reimbursement of the reasonable expenses related to the assignment, such as the cost of a credit check[1][2].
    • The assignment must be free: a tenant who assigns their lease cannot demand any sum from the assignee, nor require them to buy furniture[1].
    • In a sublet, the sublease rent cannot exceed the rent paid, plus services and reasonable fees for any furniture provided[1].

    According to the TAL, demanding consideration for an assignment or charging a sublease rent that is too high is an offence punishable by a fine[2].

    06A sample timeline

    Example: a tenant wants to assign their lease as of May 1.

    • Early March: they send the assignment notice with the assignee’s name and address and the date of May 1.
    • 15 days after the notice is received: the landlord’s response period ends.
    • With no response or if the landlord agrees: the assignee becomes the tenant on May 1.
    • Refusal for another reason: the lease ends on May 1; the landlord can re-rent.

    07The assignee’s rent

    The assignee takes over the lease as is. They are not a new tenant: they cannot have their rent set by the TAL on the ground that the rent is higher than the lowest rent paid in the last 12 months[2].

    If the lease is terminated because the landlord refused for another reason, the landlord then signs a new lease and must give the new tenant the notice of the lowest rent paid in the previous 12 months (section G of the lease)[1].

    08Recourse to the TAL

    The tenant can ask the TAL to rule on the reasons for the refusal. According to the decision summaries published by the TAL, the tribunal can, among other things, declare the assignment valid or award damages[5].

    The landlord must respond in writing, within 15 days, stating which of the three responses they choose. For the buildings we manage, every assignment notice is logged as soon as it is received and the response goes out within the deadline. See our property management.

    09Frequently asked questions

    01Can my landlord refuse my assignment without a reason?

    Yes, since February 21, 2024, but the lease then ends on the assignment date stated in your notice (s. 1978.2 CCQ).

    02What happens if the landlord does not respond?

    After 15 days from receipt of the notice, the landlord is deemed to have consented to the assignment (s. 1871 CCQ).

    03Can I ask the assignee for money?

    No. The assignment must be free (s. 1978.3 CCQ); according to the TAL, it is an offence punishable by a fine.

    04Can the landlord charge me fees?

    Only the reasonable expenses related to the assignment, such as a credit check (s. 1872 CCQ).
