    Town profile

    Renting an apartment in Berthier-sur-Mer.

    Berthier-sur-Mer stretches along the south shore of the St. Lawrence, about 6 km long and 2.5 km deep. There are farms inland and year-round and seasonal homes along the river. The roadside rest area offers a view of the river, the L’Isle-aux-Grues archipelago and Mont Sainte-Anne.

    The beach stretches over more than a kilometre of sand, and the marina hosts 65 seasonal members. Nicknamed “Sailing Capital” since 1992, the municipality gains nearly 1,000 residents in summer. Cruises to Grosse-Île, a former quarantine station, and to the L’Isle-aux-Grues archipelago leave from its marina.

    • Housing

      Single-family houses, including many year-round and seasonal homes along the river, and farms inland.

    • Nearby

      Roadside rest area with a view of the river · Parc Jean-Lesage · Cruises to Grosse-Île

    • Good to know

      The village is about 35 minutes from the Québec City bridges. Route 132 (Boulevard Blais) crosses it, and the Route verte passes through the centre of the village.

    History

    The history of Berthier-sur-Mer.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1672

      //

      In 1672, Captain Alexandre Berthier, of the Carignan regiment, received a seigneury split off from that of Bellechasse. As he also named a second seigneury upstream Berthier, today’s Berthierville, the first became Berthier-en-Bas. The seigneury then passed to the Bergères de Rigauville family.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1719 – 1720

      //

      A first stone church was built in 1719, and Bishop de Saint-Vallier established the parish of Notre-Dame-de-l’Assomption in the early 1720s. The residents lived from farming and navigation, and several captains lived in the village. Its sailors, known for their tenacity, were nicknamed the Casques de fer (Iron Helmets).

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1853 – 1873

      //

      A wharf was built in 1853. The municipality was created in 1855 under the name Notre-Dame-de-l’Assomption-de-Bellechasse, but common usage kept Berthier-en-Bas. The current church, designed by Louis-Thomas Berlinguet, was completed in 1859, away from the river and erosion. A model of a three-masted ship, donated by the village’s sailors, has hung from its vault since 1873.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1940 – 1971

      //

      From the 1940s to the 1960s, the wharf served as home port to the schooners carrying pulpwood to Québec City. In 1971, the municipality adopted the name Berthier-sur-Mer to distinguish itself from Berthierville. The word mer (sea) evokes the width of the river, its salt water and its strong tides. Former Premier Jean Lesage spent his summers there, and a park bears his name.

    01Where is Berthier-sur-Mer?

    On the south shore of the St. Lawrence, in the Montmagny MRC in Chaudière-Appalaches, about 60 km east of Québec City. The village faces the northeastern tip of Île d’Orléans.

    02How do I get to Grosse-Île from Berthier-sur-Mer?

    Guided cruises leave from the Berthier-sur-Mer marina, at 100 Rue de la Marina, from May to October. Grosse-Île served as a quarantine station from 1832 to 1937, and the visit is led by Parks Canada guides.

    03Why the name Berthier-sur-Mer?

    Berthier recalls Alexandre Berthier, first seigneur in 1672. The current name, adopted in 1971, distinguishes the village from Berthierville. Mer (sea) evokes the width of the river, its salinity and its tides at this spot.
