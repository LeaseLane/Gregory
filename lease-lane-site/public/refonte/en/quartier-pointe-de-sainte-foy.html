    Neighbourhood profile

    Rent an apartment in Pointe-de-Sainte-Foy.

    Pointe-de-Sainte-Foy is the western part of the former city of Sainte-Foy. Perched on the Québec City plateau, the neighbourhood offers views from its slopes of the St. Lawrence River, the Cap Rouge River valley and the Laurentians. Boulevard Pie-XII, Chemin des Quatre-Bourgeois, Chemin Sainte-Foy, Chemin Saint-Louis and Boulevard du Versant-Nord are its main arteries.

    In the 2021 census, the neighbourhood had 14,105 residents, and 47.5% of households were tenants. It includes the Marly, Compagnons-de-Cartier and Neilson woods, as well as Campanile and Saint-Benoît parks. The neighbourhood is also home to the head office of Revenu Québec, Saint-Benoît-Abbé church and the Collège des Compagnons.

    • Housing

      Mostly apartments and condos, with a few streets of houses, including the red-brick ones on Rue Pollack.

    • Nearby

      Parc de la Plage-Jacques-Cartier · Tracel de Cap-Rouge · Vieux-Cap-Rouge

    • Good to know

      The neighbourhood has a 1950s and 1960s sector around Boulevard Pie-XII and a more recent one: compare the two before choosing.

    History

    The history of Pointe-de-Sainte-Foy.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1698

      Parish of Sainte-Foy established

    2. 1949

    3. 1963

      Saint-Benoît parish founded

    4. 1976

      Construction of the Marly building

    5. 2002

      Merger with the City of Québec

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1638 – 1719

      //

      Under the French Regime, the territory was part of Sainte-Foy, where the Jesuits founded the Notre-Dame-de-Foy mission as early as 1638. By 1667, about twenty families were settled along what is now Chemin Sainte-Foy. The parish of Sainte-Foy was established in 1698, and its first stone church was built in 1719. The area remained agricultural, about eight kilometres from the fortifications of Québec City.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1855 – 1940

      //

      In 1855, the parish municipality of Sainte-Foy was established. Its boundaries were reduced in 1872 with the creation of the municipality of Saint-Félix-du-Cap-Rouge to the west. During the 19th century, notables and businessmen acquired estates and ran farms in the territory of Sainte-Foy, which kept a rural character until the 1940s.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1949 – 1967

      //

      Sainte-Foy became a city in 1949, and the first wave of residential construction arrived in the 1950s. Around Boulevard Pie-XII, the Pointe was built up in the 1950s and 1960s. Saint-Benoît parish was founded on June 21, 1963, and its church, designed by architect Louis Carrier, was built in 1966–1967.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1976 – 2021

      //

      In 1976, the all-glass Marly building was erected in the heart of the Marly woods; it now houses Revenu Québec. The Campanile project was added in the last fifteen years of the 20th century, on a formerly wooded area. In 2002, Sainte-Foy was merged with Québec City, and the neighbourhood’s population grew from 11,760 to 14,105 between 2001 and 2021.

    01Where is Pointe-de-Sainte-Foy?

    At the western tip of the Québec City hill, in the former city of Sainte-Foy, in the Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge borough. The Cap-Rouge neighbourhood lies to the west.

    02Where does the name Pointe-de-Sainte-Foy come from?

    The neighbourhood occupies the western end of the Québec City hill, which forms a point toward Cap-Rouge.

    03What woods and parks are there in Pointe-de-Sainte-Foy?

    The Boisé de Marly, the Boisé des Compagnons-de-Cartier, the Boisé Neilson, Parc du Campanile and Parc Saint-Benoît. Parc de la Plage-Jacques-Cartier, on the riverfront, is very close.
