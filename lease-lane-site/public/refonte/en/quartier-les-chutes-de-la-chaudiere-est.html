    Borough profile

    Renting an apartment in the Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Est borough.

    Created on January 1, 2002, the Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Est borough groups four areas: Charny, Sainte-Hélène-de-Breakeyville, Saint-Jean-Chrysostome and Saint-Romuald. The Rivière Chaudière borders it to the west and the Rivière Etchemin to the east, while Saint-Romuald touches the river to the north. Lévis City Hall is in Saint-Romuald, at 2175 Chemin du Fleuve.

    According to the City of Lévis, the borough has 49,845 people on 120 km². It has rocky outcrops, woodlots, part of the agricultural zone and Parc des Chutes-de-la-Chaudière, where the river drops 35 metres. Saint-Jean-Chrysostome also has a civil aviation airport.

    • Housing

      Older houses near the river in Saint-Romuald, large single-family neighbourhoods and rental buildings along the main routes.

    • Nearby

      Lévis City Hall · Centre hospitalier Paul-Gilbert · Charny station

    • Good to know

      The Pont de Québec and Pont Pierre-Laporte are very close, west of Saint-Romuald. Autoroute 20 crosses the borough, Charny has a VIA Rail station and the Société de transport de Lévis serves all four areas.

    History

    The history of Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Est.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1828

      //

      The territory was first part of the Lauzon seigneury. Before the parish was established, Saint-Jean-Chrysostome was called Taniata, an Abenaki word said to mean “where poplars grow.” The parish was established in 1828, the fourth separated from Saint-Joseph-de-la-Pointe-de-Lévy.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1855 – 1859

      //

      In the 19th century, the timber trade sustained the area. Saint-Romuald separated from Saint-Jean-Chrysostome in 1855, and its Chaudière-Bassin district became known for timber and shipbuilding. In 1859, Hans Denaston Breakey built a sawmill just upstream of the Chaudière Falls, on a site then called Chaudière Mills.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1854 – 1919

      //

      The first train on the Quebec & Richmond Railway line, which passes through Charny, ran on November 13, 1854. The place, called Chaudiere Junction, became an important railway hub, and the parish of Charny was founded in 1903. Breakeyville followed in 1909, around the Breakey family’s mill. The Pont de Québec, which starts in Saint-Romuald, was inaugurated in 1919.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1965 – 2002

      //

      In 1965, Saint-Romuald merged with Saint-Télesphore, and Charny and Saint-Jean-Chrysostome obtained city status. The Centre hospitalier Paul-Gilbert opened its doors in Charny in 1987. On January 1, 2002, the four municipalities were merged into Lévis, and the former Saint-Romuald town hall became that of the new city.

    01Which areas are part of Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Est?

    Four areas of Lévis: Charny, Sainte-Hélène-de-Breakeyville, Saint-Jean-Chrysostome and Saint-Romuald. The borough extends between the Rivière Chaudière and the Rivière Etchemin, from the river southward.

    02Where can you see the Chaudière Falls?

    At Parc des Chutes-de-la-Chaudière, accessible from exit 130 of Autoroute 73. A 113-metre suspension footbridge, 23 metres above the river, offers a view of the 35-metre-high falls.

    03How do I get to Québec City from Saint-Romuald or Charny?

    By the Pont de Québec, which starts in Saint-Romuald, or by the nearby Pont Pierre-Laporte. Charny also has a VIA Rail station on the Montréal–Maritimes line.
