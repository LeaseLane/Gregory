    Town profile

    Rent an apartment in Pont-Rouge.

    Pont-Rouge is part of the Portneuf MRC, in the Capitale-Nationale region. The Jacques-Cartier River runs through the town, and the aux Pommes and Noire rivers flow through its southern part. Its neighbours are Saint-Raymond to the northwest, Donnacona to the south and Saint-Augustin-de-Desmaures to the east.

    In the 2021 census, the town had 10,121 residents, up from 9,240 in 2016. Its territory covers 123.4 km². Its residents are called Pont-Rougeois (men) and Pont-Rougeoises (women).

    • Housing

      Mostly single-family houses and semi-detached homes, with duplexes and a few apartment buildings near the centre.

    • Nearby

      Rivière aux Pommes · Rue Dupont · Donnacona

    • Good to know

      Pont-Rouge borders Saint-Augustin-de-Desmaures, which makes trips to Québec City easier. A car remains the most practical way to get around.

    History

    The history of Pont-Rouge.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1867

      Parish of Sainte-Jeanne founded

    2. 1874

      Canadian Pacific railway arrives

    3. 1911

      Village and parish separate

    4. 1978

      Moulin Marcoux recognized as a historic monument

    5. 1996

      Amalgamation into the Ville de Pont-Rouge

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1769 – 1867

      //

      The first settlement efforts in the area are said to date from 1769. On April 15, 1867, the Archbishop of Québec, Charles-François Baillargeon, founded the parish of Sainte-Jeanne-de-Neuville. It was formed from parts of Cap-Santé, Saint-Basile and Neuville.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1874

      //

      Bridges and the railway shaped the area’s development. The Royal Bridge, now the Pont Déry, was a toll bridge used to carry heavy loads and mail between Québec City and Montréal. A second, free bridge was built: the Red Bridge. The Canadian Pacific railway arrived in 1874.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1870 – 1918

      //

      Around 1870 to 1872, a water mill was built of limestone on the banks of the Jacques-Cartier. This mill, the Marcoux mill, operated until it went bankrupt in 1910, then became a theatre and movie house in 1918. In 1911, the parish split in two: the village of Pont-Rouge and the parish of Sainte-Jeanne-de-Pont-Rouge.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1970 – 1996

      //

      In the 1970s, the Marcoux mill was threatened with demolition. It was restored from 1974 to 1980 and recognized as a historic monument in 1978. In 1996, the village and the parish amalgamated under the name Ville de Pont-Rouge.

    01Where is Pont-Rouge?

    In the Portneuf MRC, in the Capitale-Nationale region, on the banks of the Jacques-Cartier River. The town neighbours Donnacona to the south and Saint-Augustin-de-Desmaures to the east.

    02What is the Marcoux mill?

    A former stone water mill, built around 1870 to 1872 on the banks of the Jacques-Cartier River, at 1 Boulevard Notre-Dame. Restored from 1974 to 1980, it has been recognized as a historic monument since 1978.

    03Where does the name Pont-Rouge come from?

    It recalls the Red Bridge, a free bridge built next to the Royal Bridge, now the Pont Déry, which charged a toll. Rue Dupont, for its part, honours Father Charles-François Dupont, parish priest from 1917 to 1933.
