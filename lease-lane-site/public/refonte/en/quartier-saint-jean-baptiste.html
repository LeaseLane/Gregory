    Neighbourhood profile

    Rent an apartment in Saint-Jean-Baptiste.

    Saint-Jean-Baptiste is on the north side of the Québec City hill, at the top of the coteau Sainte-Geneviève, west of Old Québec, east of Montcalm and south of Saint-Roch. At 0.67 km², it is the smallest neighbourhood in the city, but also the densest. Rue Saint-Jean, lined with shops, bars and cafés, crosses it from east to west; Boulevard René-Lévesque brings together large hotels, office buildings and institutions.

    In the 2016 census, 77.1% of households were tenants, and 61.4% of dwellings dated from 1960 or earlier. The neighbourhood is home to the Grand Théâtre de Québec, the Théâtre Périscope, the Québec City Convention Centre and the Claire-Martin library, housed in the former St. Matthew church. Its green spaces are small parks, such as Berthelot, Richelieu and de l’Amérique-Française parks, or St-Matthew park, a former Protestant cemetery.

    • Housing

      Two- or three-storey brick houses, flat-roofed apartment buildings and housing cooperatives, in a housing stock that is mostly pre-1960.

    • Nearby

      Porte Saint-Jean · Colline Parlementaire · Avenue Cartier

    • Good to know

      Five stairways and the Faubourg elevator connect the neighbourhood to Saint-Roch, in Lower Town. Handy for getting around on foot between Upper Town and Lower Town.

    History

    The history of Saint-Jean-Baptiste.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1667

      Chemin Saint-Jean laid out

    2. 1775

      Suburb razed by the British army

    3. 1845

      Great fire of the suburb

    4. 1884

      New Saint-Jean-Baptiste church

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1667 – 1775

      //

      The Saint-Jean suburb dates back to the French Regime, west of the fortified Upper Town. Today’s Rue Saint-Jean was laid out as a road in 1667, on land largely held by the Ursulines and the Hospitalières de l’Hôtel-Dieu. A hamlet of craftsmen began to be subdivided there in 1722 and had about thirty houses by 1740. In 1775, during the American invasion, the British army had the suburb razed to clear the fortifications.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1780 – 1840

      //

      The suburb was rebuilt after 1780. In 1783, the nuns of the Hôtel-Dieu entrusted the subdivision of their lands to surveyor Vondenvelden, who laid out streets at right angles. The Berthelot market, opened in 1825, gave the suburb greater independence, and the population exceeded 9,000 residents by 1840. From the 1830s, families of Irish origin settled in the neighbouring Saint-Louis suburb, around Rues Saint-Patrick, O’Connell and Scott.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1845 – 1900

      //

      Three major fires ravaged the area in the 19th century. On June 28, 1845, nearly 1,300 houses burned; in 1876, about 500 more were lost; in 1881, fire again razed the suburb and its church. The church was rebuilt in 1884 from plans by architect Joseph-Ferdinand Peachy. Houses were then rebuilt in brick, and the flat-roofed building appeared around 1900 to make room for third-floor units.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1921 – 1980

      //

      The neighbourhood exceeded 23,000 residents in 1921. In the 1950s and 1960s, the modernization of Parliament Hill transformed the former Saint-Louis suburb completely, and thousands of residents left for the suburbs. The 1970s and 1980s saw dozens of housing and food co-operatives emerge, including the Faux-Bourg co-op, founded in 1976. Shared streets, such as Rue Sainte-Claire, now calm traffic there.

    01Where is the Saint-Jean-Baptiste neighbourhood?

    In Upper Town Québec City, between Old Québec and Montcalm, above the coteau Sainte-Geneviève that separates it from Saint-Roch. It is part of the La Cité-Limoilou borough.

    02Why is it also called the Saint-Jean suburb?

    The neighbourhood covers the former faubourg Saint-Jean, which arose under the French Regime along Chemin Saint-Jean, outside the Saint-Jean Gate. It also takes in the former faubourg Saint-Louis, south of Rue Saint-Patrick.

    03How do you walk to Saint-Roch from Saint-Jean-Baptiste?

    By the stairs of the coteau Sainte-Geneviève, including the Lavigueur, Badelard, du Faubourg, de la Chapelle and Lépine stairs, or by the Faubourg elevator.
