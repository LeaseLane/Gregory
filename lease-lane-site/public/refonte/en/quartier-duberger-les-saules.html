    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Duberger–Les Saules.

    Duberger–Les Saules is one of the five neighbourhoods of the Les Rivières borough. It is roughly bounded by Autoroute Félix-Leclerc, the Saint-Charles River and Boulevard de l’Auvergne to the north, Autoroute Henri-IV to the west, Autoroute Charest to the south and the line of Avenue Saint-Sacrement to the east. Boulevards Wilfrid-Hamel and Père-Lelièvre run through it from east to west; the Saint-Charles, Du Berger and Lorette rivers flow through it.

    In the 2021 census, the neighbourhood had 26,780 residents and 54.4% of households were tenants. It combines residential, commercial and industrial uses, including part of the Parc technologique du Québec métropolitain. It has the linear park of the Saint-Charles and Du Berger rivers, Parc de Duberger and the Jean-Baptiste-Duberger and Romain-Langlois libraries.

    • Housing

      Mostly apartments in buildings of fewer than five storeys, along with bungalows and single-family houses built after the war.

    • Nearby

      Carrefour Les Saules · Bibliothèque Romain-Langlois · Terminus Les Saules

    • Good to know

      Four autoroutes pass nearby, and the Réseau de transport de la Capitale’s Les Saules terminal serves the area.

    History

    The history of Duberger–Les Saules.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1902

      Creation of the municipality of La Petite-Rivière

    2. 1945

      Founding of the parish of Sainte-Monique-des-Saules

    3. 1960

      Sainte-Monique-des-Saules becomes Ville Les Saules

    4. 1970

      Duberger and Les Saules annexed to Québec City

    5. 2002

      Creation of the Duberger–Les Saules neighbourhood

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1855 – 1893

      //

      The territory of Duberger, originally called La Petite-Rivière, was part of the municipality of Saint-Roch-de-Québec in 1855, then, in 1893, of the new municipality of Saint-Malo. It was then an agricultural area, with houses lined up along the rangs of Petite-Rivière Sud and Nord, today’s Boulevards Wilfrid-Hamel and Père-Lelièvre. Les Saules, for its part, came under L’Ancienne-Lorette; its name comes from the many willows that line the Saint-Charles and Lorette rivers.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1902 – 1950

      //

      On March 26, 1902, the municipality of La Petite-Rivière was created; its territory roughly corresponds to the present-day neighbourhoods of Duberger and Vanier. In 1916, its eastern part split off to form Québec-Ouest, the future city of Vanier. In the early 1950s, western La Petite-Rivière still had about twenty farms, linked to Québec City and L’Ancienne-Lorette by Chemin Saint-Charles Nord, today’s Boulevard Père-Lelièvre.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1940 – 1960

      //

      In Les Saules, the parish of Sainte-Monique-des-Saules was created in 1945. From the 1940s, housing cooperatives backed by the Ligue ouvrière catholique, then by the Catholic unions, built homes there for working-class families; the cooperative’s 150th home was blessed on November 18, 1956. The municipality of Sainte-Monique-des-Saules was born in 1953 and became Ville Les Saules in 1960. Its church was completed in 1958.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1955 – 2002

      //

      La Petite-Rivière became a town in 1955. More than 1,000 bungalows were built there and its population rose from 740 to 8,500 between 1955 and 1966; Parc Boudreau and Boulevard Central were laid out according to a plan by the urban planner Jean-Claude La Haye. The town took the name Duberger in 1964. In 1970, Duberger and Les Saules were annexed to Québec City; since 2002 they have formed the Duberger–Les Saules neighbourhood.
