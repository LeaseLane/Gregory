    How much does a vacant unit really cost?

    The day-by-day calculation using CMHC figures for the Québec City region, and the levers that shorten the rental period.

    01The short answer

    A vacant unit costs roughly the monthly rent divided by 30.4, multiplied by the number of empty days, plus the costs the owner pays during the vacancy: heating, electricity, advertising, cleaning. With the average rent for a two-bedroom unit in the Québec City region, $1,277 according to CMHC[1], each empty day costs about $42 in lost rent.

    02The calculation, day by day

    The formula: monthly rent ÷ 30.4 × vacant days, plus the costs borne by the owner during the vacancy. The figure 30.4 is the average number of days in a month.

    An empty month represents 8.3% of the unit’s annual gross income (one month out of twelve), not counting costs.

    03The Québec City rental market in numbers

    • Vacancy rate in the Québec City metropolitan area in October 2025: 2.4%[1].
    • Average rent for a two-bedroom unit: $1,277, up 6.1%[1].
    • Increase of about 10% for units that changed tenants[1].
    • City of Québec only: 2.2% vacancy in 2025; the City puts the balance point at around 3%[2].

    The rise in the vacancy rate affects mainly certain areas, including the South Shore, and new units[1]. A well-located, well-priced unit still rents quickly; a poorly presented or overpriced unit stays empty longer than in 2024.

    04A worked example

    Example based on the average rent of a two-bedroom unit ($1,277), or $42 a day. The costs of $150 a month (heating, electricity, advertising) are an illustrative assumption, to be replaced with your own figures.

    Estimated cost of a vacant unit by length of vacancy (example)

    Vacant daysLost rentCosts (assumption)Total

    For comparison: lowering the rent by $25 a month to rent 30 days sooner costs $300 over a year, versus about $1,410 for an empty month.

    05The levers that shorten a vacancy

    • A market price from the day of listing: a rent that is too high is often corrected only after several lost weeks.
    • Viewings during the notice period: once the outgoing tenant has given notice that they will not renew, they must allow viewings and signs between 9 a.m. and 9 p.m. For a viewing by a prospective tenant, the landlord does not have to give 24 hours’ notice[3].
    • A quick response to every inquiry: a prospective tenant who gets no answer the same day may view elsewhere.
    • Good photos and a complete listing: inclusions, services, parking, pets.
    • The right timing: in Québec, many leases end on June 30; planning the re-rental as early as winter avoids missing the season.

    06Renting quickly, but lawfully

    To fill a unit quickly, it can be tempting to screen applicants. Several criteria are nevertheless prohibited:

    • refusing a person because they are pregnant or have children, unless the size of the dwelling justifies it[3];
    • refusing a person because they receive social assistance: this is discrimination based on social condition[4];
    • applying a rigid rent-to-income ratio, which can constitute indirect discrimination according to the Commission des droits de la personne[4].

    References from former landlords and proof of rent payment are acceptable ways to check an application[4]. See pre-rental screening.

    07Frequently asked questions

    01What is the vacancy rate in Québec City?

    2.4% in the Québec City metropolitan area in October 2025, according to CMHC. That is still below the balance point of about 3%.

    02How much does an empty month cost?

    About one twelfth of the unit’s annual gross income, or 8.3%, plus ongoing costs such as heating and electricity.

    03Can I refuse a family with children?

    No, unless the size of the dwelling justifies it (s. 1899 CCQ).

    04Does the outgoing tenant have to allow viewings?

    Yes, from the time of their non-renewal notice, between 9 a.m. and 9 p.m. The landlord does not have to give 24 hours’ notice for a viewing by a prospective tenant (ss. 1930 to 1932 CCQ).
