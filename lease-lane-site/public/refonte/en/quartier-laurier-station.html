    Town profile

    Renting an apartment in Laurier-Station.

    At about 12 km², Laurier-Station is the smallest municipality in the Lotbinière MRC by area and the most densely populated. It developed on both sides of Autoroute 20, where the railway crosses Route 271. The village combines a commercial and industrial zone along the highway with residential areas, surrounded by farmland.

    According to the Québec government’s 2026 population decree, it has 2,841 residents. It has a CLSC, two pharmacies, a dental clinic and the Complexe Récréatif, with its aquatic activities. Trains still pass through the station, rebuilt after 1918.

    • Housing

      Single-family homes, semi-detached houses and many apartments in small buildings, several of them in recent neighbourhoods. At the 2021 census, nearly one household in three was a tenant.

    • Nearby

      Complexe Récréatif · CLSC de Laurier-Station · École de la Source

    • Good to know

      Express Lotbinière buses stop on Rue Jacques and near the chapel, then continue to Saint-Apollinaire, where a line leads to the STLévis network. The Laurier-Station CLSC serves a large part of the MRC.

    History

    The history of Laurier-Station.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

      Railway line inaugurated

    4. 1945

      First furniture factory

    5. 1951

      Incorporation of the municipality

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1895

      //

      The area was first part of the parish of Saint-Flavien. In 1895, according to local history, farmer Thomas Caron convinced the superintendent of the Drummond County Railway to route the line farther north, across the lands of an area that would be called La Station. This detour became known as the “Croche à Caron.”

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1896 – 1911

      //

      The railway line was inaugurated on March 1, 1898, and a station was built there. It was named Laurier, in honour of Sir Wilfrid Laurier, Prime Minister of Canada from 1896 to 1911, who is said to have stopped there during an election campaign. In 1906, Mrs. Théophile Daigle, born Octavie Lemay, and her family built a sawmill near the railway.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1918 – 1945

      //

      The first station was destroyed in a derailment in July 1918, then rebuilt. The furniture industry gradually took hold, and the first furniture factory, Meubles Laurier, was built in 1945. Businesses and services followed.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1951 – 1960

      //

      The area separated from Saint-Flavien and became the municipality of Laurier-Station on January 1, 1951, and the new chapel held its first mass on January 28 that year. In the early 1960s, the Trans-Canada Highway, now Autoroute Jean-Lesage, drew new businesses, industries and government services. The small village became a regional crossroads.

    01Where is Laurier-Station?

    On the South Shore, in the centre of the Lotbinière MRC, in Chaudière-Appalaches. The municipality is 43 km southwest of Québec City, along Autoroute Jean-Lesage (20).

    02Where does the name Laurier-Station come from?

    From the railway station, named in honour of Sir Wilfrid Laurier, Prime Minister of Canada from 1896 to 1911. According to tradition, he stopped there during an election campaign. Its residents are called Laurierlois and Laurierloises.

    03How do I get to Québec City from Laurier-Station without a car?

    Express Lotbinière buses connect Laurier-Station to Saint-Apollinaire on weekdays. From there, the orange line joins the STLévis network, with connections to Québec City, for example to Université Laval.
