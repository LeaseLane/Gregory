    2026 rent increase: deadlines, calculation and recourse to the TAL

    The notice windows, the base rate published by the TAL and what each party can do, step by step.

    01The short answer

    For a 12-month lease, the landlord sends the rent increase notice 3 to 6 months before the lease ends[1]. The tenant has one month to respond; silence counts as acceptance[2]. If the tenant refuses, the landlord can ask the TAL to set the rent, using the calculation method published each year[3].

    02Notice deadlines

    The notice of change must be in writing and state the new rent, in dollars or as a percentage, as well as the proposed lease term[1].

    Deadlines for sending the notice, by lease term

    Lease termSend the notice
    12 months or more3 to 6 months before the lease ends
    Less than 12 months1 to 2 months before the lease ends
    Indeterminate term1 to 2 months before the change

    What the notice must contain

    • The new rent, in dollars, or the increase, in dollars or as a percentage.
    • The proposed lease term, if it changes.
    • Any other change to the conditions, for example a service removed or added.
    • The time the tenant has to refuse, which is one month after receipt.

    The notice must be written in the language of the lease. Keep proof of receipt: registered mail, a bailiff or hand delivery against a signature. In a dispute, the date of receipt is what counts, not the date of sending[1].

    03Calculating the increase

    Since January 1, 2026, the calculation follows a new method. The base is the three-year average of the Québec consumer price index, published by the TAL. Added to it, where applicable, are an adjustment for municipal and school taxes and for insurance, when their increase exceeds that base percentage; major work expenses, at the rate of 5%; and the addition of a service, accessory or dependency. Government assistance received is taken into account. A notice given before January 1, 2026 follows the old method. The result is a reference: the parties can agree on a different amount[3].

    Each year the TAL publishes the base percentage and an online calculation tool: use the version that matches the year covered by the notice[3].

    The calculation relies on supporting documents: municipal and school tax bills, insurance premiums, invoices for major work and invoices for added services or accessories. Gather them before sending the notice. An increase backed by verifiable figures is easier to discuss and easier to defend before the TAL.

    04A sample timeline

    Take a 12-month lease that ends on June 30. The dates below follow directly from the Civil Code deadlines[1][2].

    • January 1 to March 31: the landlord sends the notice of change.
    • Within the month following receipt: the tenant accepts, refuses or announces their departure. With no response, the increase is accepted.
    • Within the month following a refusal: the landlord can ask the TAL to set the rent.
    • July 1: the lease is renewed. If the TAL has not yet ruled, the old rent remains payable; the difference will be settled after the decision.

    05The tenant’s response

    • They accept: the lease is renewed at the new rent.
    • They refuse and stay: they notify the landlord in writing within the month[2].
    • They refuse and leave: they notify the landlord that they are leaving at the end of the lease.

    06Recourse to the TAL

    If the tenant refuses and stays, the landlord has one month after the response to ask the TAL to set the rent[2]. With no application, the lease is renewed on the same conditions. The TAL’s notice template avoids errors of form[4].

    Before the TAL, each party presents their documents. The tribunal applies the calculation method, can allow an increase lower or higher than the one requested, and also sets the other contested conditions.

    07Two special cases

    The new tenant

    At signing, the landlord must state the lowest rent paid in the previous 12 months, in section G of the lease. If the new rent is higher, the tenant can ask the TAL to set it within 10 days of signing, or within 2 months of the start of the lease if the information was not given or is false[5].

    The new building

    For a building built or converted into dwellings less than five years ago, the tenant cannot have the rent set by the TAL, provided this exception is entered in section F of the lease[5]. Without this mention, the usual rules apply.

    08Mistakes to avoid

    • Sending the notice too early or too late: outside the deadlines, it has no effect and the lease is renewed on the same conditions.
    • Forgetting a condition: a change missing from the notice cannot be imposed at renewal.
    • Letting the deadline for recourse pass: with no application to the TAL within the month following a refusal, the rent does not change.
    • Not keeping the supporting documents: without invoices or bills, the increase is hard to justify.

    09Frequently asked questions

    01Can the landlord raise the rent during the lease?

    No, unless the lease expressly provides for it in the case of a lease longer than 12 months. The change takes effect at renewal.

    02What happens if the tenant does not respond to the notice?

    Silence counts as acceptance of the increase: the lease is renewed on the new conditions.

    03Is the TAL rate mandatory?

    No. It serves as a reference for the calculation; the parties can agree on a different amount. In case of disagreement, the TAL sets the rent.
