    Town profile

    Renting an apartment in Sainte-Pétronille.

    Sainte-Pétronille is part of the L'Île-d'Orléans RCM, in the Capitale-Nationale region. It is the youngest of the island's six parishes, long nicknamed the Bout de l'Île. From the village, you can see Old Québec on Cap Diamant, Lévis on the south shore and, on the north shore, Montmorency Falls, 80 m high.

    In 1868, the first golf club in North America was laid out here, a three-hole course. The Église Sainte-Pétronille, built in 1871 on a promontory, has a simple interior by David Ouellet. The village also has St Mary's Chapel, a wooden Anglican chapel lit by stained-glass windows installed in 1904.

    • Housing

      Old and new houses, including large cottage-style summer residences built in the 19th century by wealthy Québec City families, along Chemin Royal.

    • Nearby

      Chocolaterie de l'île d'Orléans · St Mary's Chapel · Parc de la Chute-Montmorency

    • Good to know

      The Île d'Orléans bridge, at Saint-Pierre, connects the island to Beauport. PLUmobile public transit serves the island Monday to Friday, with connections to Québec City.

    History

    The history of Sainte-Pétronille.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1636 – 1651

      //

      The territory was part of the seigneury of Île d'Orléans, granted to Jacques Castillon on January 15, 1636. In 1651, the Jesuit Chaumonot settled refugee Wendat (Huron) there, probably near the current wharf. The following year, Éléonore de Grandmaison married Jacques Gourdeau de Beaulieu there, who gave his name to the fief.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1759 – 1820

      //

      For two centuries, the area was split between the parishes of Saint-Pierre, to the north, and Saint-Laurent, to the south. The land here is less fertile than elsewhere on the island, and was little farmed. In 1759, General Wolfe set up one of his headquarters here to conduct the siege of Québec City. Around 1820, a shipyard built two large ships there, the Colombus and the Baron Renfrew.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1938

      //

      From the mid-19th century, hundreds of Sunday strollers came by ferry. Wealthy Québec City families, including the Boswells, brewers, had summer residences built along Chemin Royal. The painter Horatio Walker worked in his studio here until his death in 1938.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1870 – 1980

      //

      In 1870, the area was detached from Saint-Pierre and erected as a parish under the name Sainte-Pétronille de Beaulieu. A village municipality was created there in 1874 under the name Beaulieu, in honour of Jacques Gourdeau de Beaulieu. It officially adopted the name Sainte-Pétronille in 1980.

    01Where is Sainte-Pétronille?

    At the southwest tip of Île d'Orléans, in the L'Île-d'Orléans RCM. The village faces Old Québec and Lévis, and borders Saint-Pierre and Saint-Laurent.

    02How do you get to Sainte-Pétronille from Québec City?

    Highway 40 leads to Beauport, where Route 368 crosses the Île d'Orléans bridge. Once on the island, at Saint-Pierre, Chemin Royal leads to the village, to the southwest.

    03Where does the name Sainte-Pétronille come from?

    Petronilla was a Roman martyr of the 1st century. According to legend, she was the daughter, real or spiritual, of Saint Peter, patron of the mother parish. The village was called Beaulieu from 1874 to 1980.
