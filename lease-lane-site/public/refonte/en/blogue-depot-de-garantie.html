    Security deposits in Québec: why they are prohibited

    What the landlord can require at signing, what they cannot ask for, and what to do if a deposit has already been paid.

    01The short answer

    In Québec, a landlord can only require the first month’s rent in advance. Any other amount is prohibited: security deposit, key deposit, last month’s rent or required postdated cheques[1][2]. A lease clause providing for a deposit has no effect[1], and the tenant can ask the TAL for a refund.

    02What the law says

    Under article 1904 of the Civil Code of Québec, the lessor can only require payment of the first rent term in advance, which is one month at most. The lessor also cannot require any sum of money other than the rent, “in the form of a deposit or otherwise,” or require the handing over of postdated cheques[1].

    This rule protects the tenant and cannot be set aside by the lease. Any clause in a dwelling lease that departs from it is without effect, even if the tenant signed it[1].

    03What the landlord can ask for, and what they cannot

    Amounts a landlord can require when a lease is signed in Québec

    RequestIn Québec
    First month’s rent, in advancePermitted, one month at most
    Last month’s rentProhibited
    Deposit for keys or the remote controlProhibited if required
    Deposit for damagesProhibited
    Postdated chequesProhibited if required; possible by mutual agreement

    The TAL specifically gives the example of keys: the lessor cannot require an additional sum “to guarantee the return of the keys.” Postdated cheques cannot be imposed, but the parties can choose this method of payment by mutual agreement[2].

    The rule in Ontario

    In Ontario, the law allows a rent deposit, often for the last month, and a refundable key deposit[4]. These practices are common elsewhere in Canada, but they do not apply to a unit located in Québec.

    04The “voluntary deposit”: a grey area?

    According to Éducaloi, a deposit is only acceptable if the tenant offers it freely and voluntarily, without any pressure[3]. In a decision reported by Éducaloi, a “voluntary deposit” form prepared by the landlord was found invalid[3].

    In practice, a document drafted by the landlord and presented as a condition of the rental is not voluntary. The tenant can refuse to pay, or pay and then ask for a refund.

    05Have you already paid a deposit?

    1. Ask the landlord for a refund in writing, citing article 1904 of the Civil Code, and keep a copy of your request.
    2. Gather your evidence: receipt, transfer, email or lease clause that mentions the deposit.
    3. If there is no refund, file an application with the Tribunal administratif du logement.

    The remedy covers the amount paid. It does not end the lease and does not justify withholding rent.

    06For owners: protecting yourself without a deposit

    • A credit check, with the applicant’s written consent. See what the landlord can ask for.
    • References from former landlords, requested from all applicants in the same way.
    • A photographed condition report at move-in and move-out, signed by both parties.
    • Recourse to the TAL for actual damages, with supporting evidence.

    07Frequently asked questions

    01Can my landlord ask for the last month’s rent?

    No. Only the first month can be required in advance (s. 1904 CCQ). A last-month deposit is permitted in Ontario, not in Québec.

    02Is a deposit for keys or the remote control permitted?

    No, if it is required. The TAL specifically cites keys as an example of a sum a landlord cannot require.

    03What should I do if I have already paid a deposit?

    Ask the landlord for a refund in writing. With no response, file an application with the TAL: the clause providing for the deposit has no effect (s. 1893 CCQ).

    04Can the landlord require postdated cheques?

    No (s. 1904 CCQ). This method of payment is possible only if both parties freely consent to it.
