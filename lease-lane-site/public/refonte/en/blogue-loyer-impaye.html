    Unpaid rent: when and how to go to the TAL

    A simple delay, more than three weeks or frequent delays: the landlord’s remedies, the 2026 fees and what never to do.

    01The short answer

    In Québec, rent is late the day after the payment date: the landlord can then claim the amounts owed from the TAL[2]. After more than three weeks of delay, the landlord can also apply to terminate the lease[1]. The tenant avoids termination by paying, before judgment, the rent owed, interest and costs[1]. The application fee is $92 since April 1, 2026[3].

    02When is rent late?

    The tenant is in default as of the day after the due date. The landlord is not required to accept a partial payment[2].

    Send a written reminder on the first day. It dates the start of the default, which matters if the case goes to the tribunal.

    03The three situations and their remedies

    The landlord’s remedies by late-rent situation

    SituationPossible remedyTo prove
    A delay, regardless of lengthRecovery of the rent, interest and costsThe amount owed
    A delay of more than three weeksTermination of the lease and recoveryThe delay of more than three weeks
    Frequent delaysTermination of the leaseThe delays and serious harm

    For a delay of more than three weeks, the TAL cannot simply order the tenant to pay in future instead of terminating[1]. For frequent delays, the landlord must show that they cause serious harm[1].

    04Filing the application with the TAL

    1. Fill out the application for non-payment of rent, online or on the TAL form[4].
    2. Pay the fee: $92 since April 1, 2026. Recipients of last-resort financial assistance are exempt[3].
    3. Wait until the application has been stamped by the TAL before serving it on the tenant[3].
    4. Serve the application on the tenant by registered mail or by bailiff, and keep proof.

    Service costs can be claimed from the tenant, according to the amounts published by the TAL[3].

    05The tenant can still pay

    The tenant avoids termination by paying, before judgment, the rent owed, interest and costs[1]. On the day of the hearing, bring an up-to-date statement of the amounts owed and the payments received.

    A written payment agreement, signed by both parties, is often the fastest way to recover the money without a hearing.

    06What the landlord can never do

    • Change the lock without the tenant’s consent[1].
    • Harass the tenant to push them to leave[1].
    • Cut off a service, such as heating or electricity, to force payment: the landlord must provide peaceable enjoyment of the dwelling for the entire term of the lease[1].
    • Require a deposit or more than one month’s rent in advance to protect against a future delay. See the security deposit.

    07After the decision

    A termination decision is enforced according to the rules published by the TAL[5]. A tenant who leaves remains responsible for the lost rent until the unit is re-rented, and the landlord must try to re-rent to limit their losses.

    08Frequently asked questions

    01When can I go to the TAL?

    From the day after the due date to claim the rent. After more than three weeks of delay, you can also apply to terminate the lease (s. 1971 CCQ).

    02Can I change the locks?

    No. The tenant’s consent is required (s. 1934 CCQ), and harassment is prohibited (s. 1902 CCQ).

    03The tenant pays everything before the hearing: what happens?

    If the tenant pays the rent, interest and costs before judgment, termination is avoided (s. 1883 CCQ).

    04How much does an application to the TAL cost?

    $92 since April 1, 2026 for an application for non-payment of rent, plus service costs.
