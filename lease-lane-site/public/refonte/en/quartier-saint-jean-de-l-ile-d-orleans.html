    Town profile

    Renting an apartment in Saint-Jean-de-l’Île-d’Orléans.

    Saint-Jean-de-l’Île-d’Orléans is part of the MRC de L’Île-d’Orléans, in the Capitale-Nationale region. It stretches 11 km, from the Maheu River to the Dauphine River, which separates it from Saint-François. It is one of the largest municipalities on the island, and the Lafleur River also runs through it.

    In the 19th century, its population made it the capital of the island. The Manoir Mauvide-Genest, built in 1734, has been a classified heritage site since 1971 and a National Historic Site of Canada since 1993. It houses an interpretation centre on the seigneurial regime. The church, built between 1734 and 1737 on the riverbank, has been classified since 1957.

    • Housing

      Houses and farms along Chemin Royal. Several old houses and barns have heritage status.

    • Nearby

      Musée maritime de l’île d’Orléans · Saint-François observation tower · Saint-François church

    • Good to know

      The village is about 26 km as the crow flies northeast of Québec City. The island is reached by the Île-d’Orléans Bridge, at Saint-Pierre, and PLUmobile offers public transit Monday to Friday.

    History

    The history of Saint-Jean-de-l’Île-d’Orléans.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1680

      Mission served by Sainte-Famille

    2. 1714

      Erection of the parish

    3. 1734

      Construction of the Manoir Mauvide-Genest

    4. 1993

      Manor designated a National Historic Site

    5. 2003

      Adoption of the present name

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1636 – 1714

      //

      The territory is part of the seigneury of Île d’Orléans, granted on January 15, 1636. A wooden chapel was built there in 1675. From 1680, Saint-Jean was a mission served by the priest of Sainte-Famille, then became a parish in 1714.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1734 – 1759

      //

      In 1734, surgeon Jean Mauvide and his wife, Marie-Anne Genest, had a stone house built, which they later expanded into a manor. Enriched by maritime trade, Mauvide bought the southwest half of the seigneury in 1752. Construction of the church also began in 1734 and was completed in 1737. In 1759, the manor was one of the few dwellings spared by Wolfe’s troops.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1845 – 1855

      //

      A municipality was created in 1845 under the name Saint-Jean, Isle d’Orléans, then the parish municipality of Saint-Jean-Baptiste in 1855. In 1852, the church was enlarged to plans by architect Louis-Thomas Berlinguet, who gave it a neoclassical façade. Saint-Jean was also a village of pilots, who guided ships on the St. Lawrence.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1912 – 2003

      //

      Jean-Charles Bonenfant, future director of the Legislature’s library, was born there in 1912. In 1926, Judge Joseph-Camille Pouliot bought the manor, then in poor condition, and restored it. The church was classified in 1957 and the manor in 1971. In 2003, the municipality took the name Saint-Jean-de-l’Île-d’Orléans.

    01Where is Saint-Jean-de-l’Île-d’Orléans?

    On the south shore of Île d’Orléans, between Saint-Laurent and Saint-François. The village is about 26 km as the crow flies northeast of Québec City.

    02What is the Manoir Mauvide-Genest?

    A seigneurial manor built in 1734 for surgeon Jean Mauvide and his wife, Marie-Anne Genest. A National Historic Site of Canada since 1993, it houses an interpretation centre on the seigneurial regime.

    03Where does the name Saint-Jean come from?

    According to the Commission de toponymie, the name may recall Jean de Lauson, Seneschal of New France, who died at the mouth of the Maheu River in 1661. It could also come from Saint John the Baptist, patron saint of the parish.
