    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Val-Bélair.

    Val-Bélair covers 69 km² in the northwest of the city, between the Châtels neighbourhood to the east and Sainte-Catherine-de-la-Jacques-Cartier to the west. To the north, it is bordered by the Laurentians and Garrison Valcartier, and Mont Bélair rises above it at 485 metres. Boulevard Pie-XI is its main artery; Route Sainte-Geneviève, Avenue Industrielle and Rue de Montolieu connect it to Autoroute Henri-IV.

    In the 2016 census, 23.2% of residents were tenants, out of a population of 28,665 and an average age of 35.1. The Base de plein air de Val-Bélair offers 55 km of cross-country ski and snowshoe trails in winter. The neighbourhood also has Parc La Chanterelle, Parc de détente Belle-Eau, the Félix-Leclerc library and the Georges-Dor cultural centre.

    • Housing

      Mostly single-family houses, with apartments in small buildings of fewer than five storeys.

    • Nearby

      Parc La Chanterelle · Félix-Leclerc Library · Corridor des Cheminots

    • Good to know

      Autoroute Henri-IV is the most direct route into the neighbourhood. Shops are concentrated on Boulevard Pie-XI, near Avenue Industrielle and Avenue de la Montagne.

    History

    The history of Val-Bélair.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1682

      Fief of Bélair granted

    2. 1909

      Parish of Saint-Gérard-Majella erected

    3. 1933

      Creation of Val-Saint-Michel

    4. 1974

      Merger of Bélair and Val-Saint-Michel

    5. 2002

      Merger with the City of Québec

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1682 – 1800

      //

      In the days of New France, the fief of Bélair was granted to Guillaume Bonhomme in 1682, and the grant was ratified by Louis XIV on April 15, 1684. The fief included Mont Bélair and Lac Bonhomme. After Guillaume Bonhomme died in 1710, his children gradually sold their shares to the Jesuits; in 1800, the territory became British Crown land.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1906 – 1933

      //

      The parish of Saint-Gérard-Majella was founded in 1906 and canonically erected in 1909; a parish municipality of the same name was created the same year. Residents then made their living from the timber industry and hunting. In 1914, the first cottages appeared on the road that would become Boulevard Pie-XI, and the resort municipality of Val-Saint-Michel was created in 1933.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1939 – 1977

      //

      From 1939, the Second World War caused the population to grow, as many residents worked at the nearby military base and munitions factories. In 1965, Saint-Gérard-Majella became a city and took the name Bélair, recalling the former seigneury. On January 1, 1974, Bélair and Val-Saint-Michel merged to form Val-Bélair, which was briefly placed under trusteeship in 1977.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1971 – 2009

      //

      In the early 1990s, the city grew strongly under the slogan "Val-Bélair, ville de la nature." Its population rose from 11,000 in 1971 to more than 22,000 in 2002. On January 1, 2002, Val-Bélair was merged with Québec City; first part of the Laurentien borough, it has been part of La Haute-Saint-Charles since 2009. In 2004, remaining in Québec City won narrowly in a referendum, with 50.24% of the vote.

    01Where is Val-Bélair?

    In the northwest of Québec City, in the La Haute-Saint-Charles borough, between the Châtels neighbourhood and Sainte-Catherine-de-la-Jacques-Cartier, at the foot of the Laurentians.

    02Where does the name Val-Bélair come from?

    It combines the names of Bélair and Val-Saint-Michel, which merged in 1974. Bélair recalls the fief of Bélair, granted to Guillaume Bonhomme in 1682.

    03How do I get to Val-Bélair?

    Autoroute Henri-IV is the most direct route. Route Sainte-Geneviève, Avenue Industrielle and Rue de Montolieu lead to Boulevard Pie-XI, and the Corridor des Cheminots, a bike path, connects the neighbourhood to downtown.
