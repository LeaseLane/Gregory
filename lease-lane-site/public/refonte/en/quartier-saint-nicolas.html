    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Saint-Nicolas.

    Saint-Nicolas is one of the three sectors of the Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Ouest borough. Set on a promontory overlooking the river, it extends about 19 km along the St. Lawrence, to the Chaudière River in the east. Three residential zones can be distinguished: Bernières and the bridges in the east, the Stastny golf area in the centre and the old village in the west.

    At the 2006 census, Saint-Nicolas had 18,437 residents over about 95 km². City-wide, 34.1% of Lévis households were tenants in 2021. The area has five elementary schools and the école secondaire de l’Envol. The heritage site preserves the Maison Pâquet, built around 1760, and the Chapelle Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, from 1867-1868.

    History

    The history of Saint-Nicolas.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1694

      Canonical erection of the parish

    2. 1721

      Construction of a stone church

    3. 1962

      New church and city status

    4. 1987

      Designation of the heritage site

    5. 2002

      Merger into the City of Lévis

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1660 – 1694

      //

      The territory is part of the vast seigneury of Lauzon and has been inhabited since 1660. The parish was founded in 1668; in 1683, it had about fifty families. In 1690, a first mill was built at the anse aux Hirondelles and a chapel was erected. The name Saint-Nicolas was assigned in 1694, at the canonical erection, at the request of Claude de Bermen de La Martinière, administrator of the seigneury.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1721 – 1845

      //

      A stone church was built in 1721. In the fall of 1759, English troops occupied the parish and soldiers moved into the church, which was badly damaged by the war. At the end of the 18th century, the place had about 700 inhabitants. The parish municipality was created in 1845; the economy then relied for a long time on agriculture, brickmaking and the exploitation of timber and stone.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1821 – 1968

      //

      In 1912, part of the territory became the municipality of Saint-Nicolas-Sud, renamed Bernières in 1968 in memory of Bernières-sur-Mer, in Normandy. The church built in 1821 was destroyed by fire on November 22, 1961. The new church, designed by architect André Gilbert, rose starting in 1962; its oval nave evokes a ship. That same year, Saint-Nicolas obtained city status.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1987 – 2002

      //

      In 1987, a group of old houses linked to the Pâquet family became the Saint-Nicolas heritage site, one of the first sites designated by a municipality in Québec. Bernières and Saint-Nicolas merged on September 21, 1994, and the new city took the name Saint-Nicolas again in 1996. On January 1, 2002, it was grouped with eight other municipalities to form the new city of Lévis.

    01Where is Saint-Nicolas?

    On the south shore of the St. Lawrence, west of the Chaudière River, in Lévis. It is one of the three sectors of the Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Ouest borough, along with Saint-Rédempteur and Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon.

    02Where does the name Saint-Nicolas come from?

    The name was assigned in 1694 at the request of Claude de Bermen de La Martinière, who wanted to recall his home parish, Saint-Nicolas-de-la-Ferté, in France. It also evokes Saint Nicholas, a 4th-century bishop.

    03What is the Saint-Nicolas heritage site?

    A small group of old houses, mostly on Route Marie-Victorin, designated in 1987. It includes the Maison Pâquet, built around 1760, and the Chapelle Notre-Dame-de-Grâce, from 1867-1868.
