    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in the Aéroport neighbourhood.

    The Aéroport neighbourhood has the most farms in Québec City, especially northwest of the airport. Its residential areas, Champigny, Jouvence and Chauveau, are in the south, between Autoroute Félix-Leclerc and Boulevard Wilfrid-Hamel, and around Boulevard Chauveau-Ouest. A big-box retail area extends to the southeast, on both sides of Autoroute Duplessis.

    In the 2021 census, the neighbourhood had 13,375 residents, and 17.4% of households were tenants. It is home to the Sainte-Foy outdoor base, the Parc des Sentiers-de-la-Rivière-du-Cap-Rouge and the Saint-Ange and Saint-Denis farming rangs. The neighbourhood also has the Collège de Champigny, the Chauveau industrial park and part of the Colbert industrial park.

    • Housing

      Mostly detached houses, with a more modest share of apartments in buildings under five storeys.

    • Nearby

      L’Ancienne-Lorette · Lac Saint-Augustin · Cap-Rouge

    • Good to know

      The neighbourhood is very large: check which area the unit is in (Champigny, Jouvence or Chauveau) and its access to Autoroutes Félix-Leclerc and Duplessis.

    History

    The history of the Aéroport.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1855

      Creation of the parish of Ancienne-Lorette

    2. 1941

      First commercial flight at the airport

    3. 1970

      Annexation to the city of Sainte-Foy

    4. 1993

      The airport becomes Jean-Lesage

    5. 2009

      Joined to Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1674 – 1697

      //

      The territory is first attached to Lorette, where the Jesuit Pierre Chaumonot built a chapel in 1674 for the Huron-Wendat who had come to take refuge near Québec City. After they left for Jeune-Lorette in 1697, the place took the name Ancienne-Lorette and was settled by French colonists. The Champigny area was known as Gaudarville, and the Chauveau area as Les Grands-Déserts.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1855 – 1947

      //

      In 1855, the territory formed the parish municipality of Ancienne-Lorette. It lost part of its territory in 1872 with the creation of Saint-Félix-du-Cap-Rouge, then in 1909 with that of Saint-Gérard-Majella. In 1947, the village separated from the parish to form the village municipality of Notre-Dame-de-Lorette, and the parish remained mostly agricultural.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1939 – 1976

      //

      In 1939, an airfield was created there to train aerial observers during the Second World War. The first commercial flight took place on September 11, 1941, and the airport became a civilian facility in 1945. Construction of the first modern terminal began in 1955, and the first transatlantic flights arrived in 1976.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1969 – 2009

      //

      On March 15, 1969, the parish took the name L’Ancienne-Lorette, then was annexed to Sainte-Foy on December 26, 1970 and became the Laurentien neighbourhood. In 1993, the airport was renamed in honour of Jean Lesage. After the 2002 merger, the Laurentien neighbourhood became the Aéroport neighbourhood, attached since November 1, 2009 to the Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge borough.

    01Where is the Aéroport neighbourhood?

    In the northwest of Québec City, in the Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge borough, around Jean-Lesage International Airport. It borders the town of L’Ancienne-Lorette.

    02Where does the name of the Aéroport neighbourhood come from?

    From Jean-Lesage International Airport, located at the heart of its territory. Before 2002, the area was called the Laurentien neighbourhood, a name many residents still use.

    03What are the residential areas of the Aéroport neighbourhood?

    Champigny, Jouvence and Chauveau. The neighbourhood also includes the farming area of the Saint-Ange and Saint-Denis rangs and a small portion of the Lac Saint-Augustin area.
