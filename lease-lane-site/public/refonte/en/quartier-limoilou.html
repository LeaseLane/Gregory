    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Limoilou.

    Vieux-Limoilou is bounded by the Rivière Saint-Charles to the south, Boulevard Wilfrid-Hamel, Avenue Eugène-Lamontagne and 18e Rue to the north, and the Laurentienne highway to the west. Its grid plan is crossed from north to south by 1re Avenue; 3e Avenue is its commercial artery, with its cafés, restaurants and boutiques.

    It is a tenant neighbourhood: at the 2016 census, 75.8% of its residents were tenants. It has the main campus of Cégep Limoilou, the Cartier-Brébeuf National Historic Site, the École de cirque de Québec and neighbourhood events such as the Envol et Macadam festival.

    • Housing

      Mostly plexes with outdoor staircases, typical of Québec City, and 4 ½ and 5 ½ units on grid streets that are easy to navigate.

    • Nearby

      Cartier-Brébeuf National Historic Site · Domaine de Maizerets · Hôpital de l’Enfant-Jésus

    • Good to know

      Parking is often on the street. Check whether the street is covered by the City’s resident parking permit.

    History

    The history of Limoilou.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1535 – 1626

      //

      In 1535, Jacques Cartier landed on the banks of the Rivière Saint-Charles, very close to present-day Limoilou. In 1626, the Notre-Dame-des-Anges seigneury was granted to the Jesuits, and the lands north of the river’s mouth became farms. This agricultural character lasted until the early 20th century.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1845 – 1890

      //

      In 1847, lumber merchant William Hedley Anderson divided his land into lots to attract workers: this was the birth of the village of Hedleyville. Three other working-class villages followed: Stadacona, Parkville and New Waterford. Overcrowding in Québec City, cholera epidemics and the fires of 1845 pushed many families toward these villages. The Lavigueur bridge, in 1867, and the arrival of the railway, in 1890, helped them grow.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1893

      //

      In 1893, after a fire ravaged part of Hedleyville, the villages joined together to form the municipality of Limoilou, named in honour of Jacques Cartier, sieur de Limoëlou. Since the territory was still mostly agricultural, developers drew inspiration from New York’s grid: streets running east to west, avenues north to south and brick residential buildings, often more than three storeys.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1909 – 1931

      //

      Annexed to Québec City in 1909, Limoilou boomed: its population rose from 3,652 in 1911 to 25,795 in 1931. The neighbourhood then spread east, to Maizerets, and north, to Lairet. Today the original area is called “Vieux-Limoilou,” one of the densest in the city.
