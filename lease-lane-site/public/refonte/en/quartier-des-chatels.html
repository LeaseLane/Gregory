    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in the Des Châtels neighbourhood.

    The Des Châtels neighbourhood is L-shaped: it includes the part of the borough west of Boulevard de l’Ormière, plus the part east of that boulevard and south of Rue Durand. The Saint-Charles River marks its southeastern boundary. Autoroute Henri-IV, Avenue Chauveau, Boulevard Bastien and Boulevard Saint-Claude run through it, and its western part still has farmland and woodland.

    In the 2016 census, 23.8% of residents were tenants; the population reached 14,450 in 2021. The neighbourhood has several parks, including Parc Chauveau, Parc Montchâtel and Parc de la Chaumière, as well as the linear park of the Saint-Charles and Du Berger rivers. It is home to the Neufchâtel library, the Michel-Labadie community centre and the École secondaire de Neufchâtel.

    • Housing

      Mostly single-family houses, with apartments in small buildings of fewer than five storeys.

    • Nearby

      Rue Racine, in Loretteville · Parc linéaire de la rivière Saint-Charles · Val-Bélair

    • Good to know

      Autoroute Henri-IV and Avenue Chauveau connect the neighbourhood to the rest of the city. Businesses are mostly along Boulevard de l’Ormière.

    History

    The history of Des Châtels.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · Chapter 01

      //

      From the end of the 17th century, Huron-Wendat settled in the region after leaving their settlement at Lorette, today’s L’Ancienne-Lorette; their Wendake reserve is a little east of the neighbourhood. Boulevard de l’Ormière follows a road dating from the French regime. The founding of the parish of Saint-Ambroise-de-la-Jeune-Lorette, in the late 18th century, marked the start of settlement in the territory.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1913 – 1950

      //

      In the mid-19th century, the territory was organized as a parish municipality under the name Saint-Ambroise-de-la-Jeune-Lorette. Over time, several village centres split off from it, including Loretteville in 1913. The remaining lands, mostly agricultural, kept the name Saint-Ambroise. Urban expansion began to reach the area in the 1950s.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1963 – 1970

      //

      On September 21, 1963, the municipality took the name Neufchâtel, in tribute to residents who fought at Neufchâtel-en-Bray, in Normandy, during the Second World War. It gained town status on July 11, 1964. Inaugurated in 1970, the building at 9150 Boulevard de l’Ormière then served as fire station and town hall.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1971 – 2002

      //

      Problems with the drinking water supply led to the annexation of Neufchâtel to Québec City on January 1, 1971. During the 2002 mergers, its territory was divided among several neighbourhoods. Its western part became the Des Châtels neighbourhood, in the La Haute-Saint-Charles borough, whose name is derived from that of Neufchâtel.

    It is derived from Neufchâtel, the former town whose western part the neighbourhood occupies. The name, adopted in 1963, pays tribute to residents who fought at Neufchâtel-en-Bray, in Normandy, during the Second World War.
