    Property management fees in Québec City: how much do they cost?

    The billing methods seen in Québec, the published ranges, the tax deduction and the questions to ask before signing.

    01The short answer

    In Québec City, managers most often charge a percentage of the rent collected. Québec firms publish ranges of roughly 4% to 11% depending on the type of building and the scope of service[1][2][3], plus leasing fees that often run from 50% to 100% of one month’s rent[1][2]. These fees are deductible from rental income[4].

    02Billing methods

    Property management billing methods seen in Québec, according to Québec firms

    ItemBilling methodPublished range
    Day-to-day managementPercentage of rent collectedAbout 4% to 7% at one firm[1]; 5% to 10% of gross income at another, 4% to 6% for multi-unit buildings[2]; 5% to 10% for residential at a third[3]
    Leasing a unitPercentage of one month’s rent50% to 100% of the first month[1][2]
    House or condoMonthly flat fee$100 to $200 a month at one firm[2]
    TAL fileFlat fee per file$150 to $300 at one firm[2]
    Supervision of workPercentage of the cost of the work10% to 15% at one firm[2]

    These ranges are the ones firms publish on their websites; they vary with the building, the number of units and what is included. They are not a guaranteed price.

    03Rent collected or rent billed?

    Check the basis of calculation. A percentage of rent collected costs nothing during a vacancy or a late payment; a percentage of rent billed is paid even when the money does not come in. Many firms that charge a percentage charge nothing on a vacant unit[2][3], but that is a firm’s practice: read the contract.

    04What should be included

    • rent collection and follow-up of late payments;
    • communication with tenants and handling of requests;
    • coordination of routine maintenance and emergencies;
    • lease renewals and notices within the deadlines;
    • a monthly report of income, expenses and open requests;
    • access to documents: leases, invoices, history.

    What is often billed on top: leasing, TAL files, supervision of major work and file-opening fees.

    05Comparing two quotes

    1. The basis of calculation: rent collected or billed?
    2. Fees during a vacancy.
    3. The length of the contract, the notice period and the termination conditions.
    4. The frequency and content of the accounting.
    5. The account where rent is deposited: separate or in trust?
    6. The response time for emergencies, at night and on weekends.
    7. Ancillary fees and taxes on top.

    A quote that is cheaper in percentage terms can cost more overall once ancillary fees are added. Ask for a worked example on your building.

    06Deductible fees

    Amounts paid to a person or company to manage your building are deductible as management and administration fees from your rental income (federal form T776)[4]. In Québec, rental income and expenses are reported on form TP-128[5].

    The net cost of management is therefore lower than the advertised percentage. Check your situation with your accountant.

    07The TAL’s 3.5% is not a fee schedule

    The TAL uses a “management fees” percentage in its rent calculation parameters. It is a regulatory parameter, not a market rate: it says nothing about what a manager charges.

    09Frequently asked questions

    01What percentage is common in Québec City?

    The ranges published by Québec firms run from roughly 4% to 11% of rent collected, depending on the building and the service.

    02Do you pay management fees during a vacancy?

    Many firms that charge a percentage of rent collected charge nothing on a vacant unit. Check the basis of calculation in the contract.

    03Are management fees deductible?

    Yes, as management and administration fees against rental income (T776 federally; TP-128 in Québec).

    04How much does it cost to lease a unit?

    Firms most often publish 50% to 100% of the first month’s rent.
