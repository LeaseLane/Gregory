    Pre-rental screening: what the landlord can ask for

    The information that is permitted and prohibited, consent to the credit check, discrimination and record retention (Law 25).

    01The short answer

    In Québec, a landlord can ask for the name, address and contact information of the applicant’s former landlords. With the applicant’s consent, the landlord can run a credit check, which requires only the name, address and date of birth[1]. The landlord cannot require the SIN, a bank account number, pay stubs, health information or copies of identity documents[1][2].

    02What is permitted, and what is not

    Information a landlord can ask a prospective tenant for in Québec

    InformationCan it be requested?
    Name, first name and addressYes[1]
    Name and contact information of former landlords or superintendentsYes[1]
    Date of birthOnly for a credit check, with consent[1][2]
    Credit checkOnly with the applicant’s consent[1]
    Viewing an identity documentYes, without copying it, photographing it or noting its numbers[1]
    SIN, health insurance, passport or driver’s licence numberNo[1][2]
    Bank account number or balance, pay stub, T4No[2]
    Health status, pregnancy, civil status, country of originNo[2]

    03The credit check, with consent

    A credit check requires the applicant’s written consent and needs only their name, address and date of birth. The SIN is therefore not necessary[1].

    An applicant who refuses the credit check can provide their own credit report, a bank statement of account, a reference letter or proof of payment of bills, for example electricity or telecommunications[1]. The landlord cannot refuse an applicant for not providing their health insurance or driver’s licence numbers[1].

    04Prohibited grounds for refusal

    The Charter of human rights and freedoms prohibits discrimination based on, among other things, pregnancy, civil status, age, ethnic or national origin and social condition[2][3]. According to the Commission des droits de la personne, refusing an applicant because they receive social assistance or because they are a student is discriminatory[2].

    The Civil Code also prohibits refusing to lease to a woman because she is pregnant or to a person because they have children, unless the size of the dwelling justifies it; punitive damages are possible[4].

    05Keeping and destroying records (Law 25)

    • Collect only the necessary information, for the purposes determined before collection[5].
    • Use it only for those purposes[5].
    • Once the purpose is fulfilled, destroy or anonymize it, subject to any retention period set by law[5]. Files of unsuccessful applicants do not have to be kept indefinitely.

    06And TAL decisions?

    TAL decisions are public and available free of charge on the SOQUIJ website[6]. If you consult them to check an applicant, the necessity principle of Law 25 applies: keep only what is useful to the decision to rent.

    07The checklist for a compliant screening

    1. An application form limited to what is necessary, with no SIN or account number, even “optional.”
    2. Separate written consent for the credit check.
    3. No copies of identity documents.
    4. The same criteria for all applicants.
    5. Restricted access to files.
    6. Destruction of files of unsuccessful applicants.

    LeaseLane’s rental application follows these rules: it collects only the necessary information and asks for consent to the credit check separately.

    08Frequently asked questions

    01Can the landlord require my SIN?

    No. According to the Commission d’accès à l’information, the SIN is not necessary for the credit check.

    02Can they photocopy my driver’s licence?

    No. They can view the identity document, but not keep a copy or note its numbers.

    03I refuse the credit check: what should I do?

    Provide your own credit report, a bank statement of account or references from former landlords.

    04Can they refuse me because I have children or receive social assistance?

    No (s. 1899 CCQ and s. 10 of the Charter), except in the case of children if the size of the dwelling justifies it.
