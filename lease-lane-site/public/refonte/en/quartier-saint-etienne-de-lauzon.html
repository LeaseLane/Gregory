    Neighbourhood profile

    Rent an apartment in Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon.

    Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon is one of the three sectors of the Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Ouest borough. Crossed by the Beaurivage River, it is bordered to the east by the Chaudière River. It touches Saint-Nicolas to the north and Saint-Rédempteur to the northeast, as well as Saint-Lambert-de-Lauzon, Saint-Gilles, Saint-Agapit and Saint-Apollinaire to the south and west.

    In the 2011 census, the sector had 9,990 residents. City-wide, 34.1% of Lévis households were tenants in 2021. It is home to the Plein-Soleil, La Chanterelle and La Clé-du-Boisé schools, the Centre Bruno-Verret arena, the Albert-Rousseau library and several parks, including Grandes-Pointes and Jean-de-Lauzon. The Maison des aînés et alternative de Lévis opened there in 2023.

    • Housing

      Mostly single-family bungalow-style houses, which made up 71% of the neighbourhood’s households in 2016.

    • Nearby

      Parc des Chutes-de-la-Chaudière · Pont Pierre-Laporte · Saint-Rédempteur

    • Good to know

      In spring, the Beaurivage often overflows its banks. Check where the unit is in relation to the river before you sign.

    History

    The history of Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    1. 1858

      Canonical erection of the parish

    2. 1862

      Baillargeon post office opens

    3. 1904

      Construction of the present church

    4. 1937

      Post office takes its present name

    5. 2002

      Merger into the City of Lévis

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1636 – 1798

      //

      The territory is part of the seigneury of Lauzon, granted in 1636 to Simon Le Maître, a front man for Jean de Lauson. The first settlers moved in near the Beaurivage and the Chaudière in the early 18th century, in the time of the seigneur Étienne Charest. Around 1780–1790, families settled the Grandes-Pointes concession, also called Longues-Pointes, and its residents asked for a chapel in 1798.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1804 – 1870

      //

      In 1804, Napoleon’s Continental Blockade pushed England to look for timber in its colonies, and logging began on the lands of the future parish. The parish was established canonically in 1858, from territories of Saint-Nicolas and Saint-Lambert, then civilly in 1860. Its name recalls Étienne Baillargeon, parish priest of Saint-Nicolas from 1838 to 1870; the post office opened in 1862 was in fact named Baillargeon.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1860 – 1917

      //

      Rich in marl, the land produced mainly wheat and buckwheat until the 1860s, then oats, potatoes and milk, sold at the Québec City and Lévis markets. In 1879, two stations served the territory, La Chaudière and Craig’s Road. The present church was built in 1904, and a bridge linked the two banks of the Beaurivage in 1917.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1919 – 2002

      //

      In 1919, Saint-Rédempteur separated from the parish, and the post office took the name Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon in 1937. The municipality’s name was shortened to Saint-Étienne in 1957, then restored in 1982. From the 1960s, Highway 20 and the automobile turned the village into a suburb of Québec City, and farmland gradually gave way to bungalows. On January 1, 2002, the municipality was amalgamated into the new city of Lévis.

    01Where is Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon?

    South of Saint-Nicolas, on both sides of the Beaurivage River, west of the Chaudière River. It is one of the three sectors of the Les Chutes-de-la-Chaudière-Ouest borough, in Lévis.

    02Where does the name Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon come from?

    It recalls Abbé Étienne Baillargeon, parish priest of Saint-Nicolas from 1838 to 1870, who worked on the creation of the parish. “Lauzon” marks the territory’s membership in the former seigneury of Lauzon.

    03What parks are there in Saint-Étienne-de-Lauzon?

    The Grandes-Pointes, Ludger-Bastien, des Aînés, Jean-de-Lauzon, Gilles-Rageot, Martin and de L’Art-de-Vivre parks.
