    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Saint-Louis.

    Saint-Louis is one of the seven neighbourhoods of the Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge borough. The Québec and Pierre-Laporte bridges lead there, and the bridgehead area is served by Autoroutes Henri-IV and Duplessis and by Boulevards Laurier, Hochelaga and Champlain. Chemin des Quatre-Bourgeois, Chemin Saint-Louis and Route de l’Église complete the network.

    At the 2021 census, the neighbourhood had 16,535 residents and 55.1% of households were tenants. It is home to the Aquarium du Québec, the Centre de glaces de Québec, the Centre sportif de Sainte-Foy and several parks, including Saint-Louis-de-France and Roland-Beaudin. The CHUL, the Andrée-P.-Boucher building, the Sainte-Foy public market and the Monique-Corriveau library are also located there.

    • Housing

      Apartments in buildings of fewer than five storeys, single-family homes and a notable share of recent construction.

    • Nearby

      Monique-Corriveau library · Sainte-Foy public market · Centre hospitalier de l’Université Laval

    • Good to know

      The Sainte-Foy station, for trains and coaches, is in the neighbourhood, as is access to the two bridges to the South Shore.

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1917 – 1950

      //

      The neighbourhood takes its name from Chemin Saint-Louis and the Saint-Louis-de-France parish. The road, laid out in the 17th and 18th centuries under the name Chemin de Cap-Rouge, took its current name in 1929. Around 1920, a settlement called Neilsonville bordered Chemin Saint-Louis, near the Québec Bridge, completed in 1917. Until the 1950s, this road was the only route linking the bridge to the capital.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1928 – 1946

      //

      In 1928, the Bois-Gomin aerodrome, or Saint-Louis aerodrome, the first in the Québec City region, was built on the Sainte-Foy plateau, in the area of today’s CHUL. Roméo Vachon, chief pilot of Canadian Transcontinental Airways, contributed to its growth. Too costly, the aerodrome closed in 1938. In 1946, the land was sold to the Department of Veterans Affairs for $43,500.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1949 – 1968

      //

      The construction of Boulevard Laurier, between 1949 and 1951, opened the area to development. The Hôpital Sainte-Foy, intended for veterans, was inaugurated on May 16, 1954; it became the Centre hospitalier de l’Université Laval in 1968. The Saint-Louis-de-France parish was founded in 1956; its church, completed in 1961 from plans by Robert Blatter and G.-Fernand Caron, was the first centralized plan in the Québec City region.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1959 – 2021

      //

      The Aquarium du Québec welcomed its first visitors in 1959. The Pierre-Laporte Bridge opened to traffic on November 7, 1970, less than 200 m west of the Québec Bridge. The Sainte-Foy city hall, today the Andrée-P.-Boucher building, was inaugurated in 1996. In 2013, the former Saint-Denys-du-Plateau church, the work of Jean-Marie Roy, became the Monique-Corriveau library; the Saint-Louis-de-France church was demolished in 2021.

    01Where is the Saint-Louis neighbourhood?

    In the Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge borough, in the south of the city, at the head of the Québec and Pierre-Laporte bridges. It should not be confused with the Saint-Louis suburb, near Old Québec.

    02Where does the name Saint-Louis come from?

    From Chemin Saint-Louis and the Saint-Louis-de-France parish, founded in 1956 and named in honour of Louis IX, King of France from 1226 to 1270.

    03How do you get to Saint-Louis?

    By Autoroutes Henri-IV and Duplessis, Boulevard Laurier or Chemin Saint-Louis. From the South Shore, the Québec and Pierre-Laporte bridges lead there, and the Sainte-Foy station serves trains and coaches.
