    Borough profile

    Renting an apartment in La Haute-Saint-Charles.

    La Haute-Saint-Charles is one of the six boroughs of Québec City. Its name comes from its position in the upper part of the Saint-Charles River watershed. It has Lac Saint-Charles and, at Château-d’Eau near Loretteville, the water intake that supplies about half of Québec City’s population. The borough also surrounds the Huron-Wendat community of Wendake.

    In the 2021 census, the borough had 88,460 residents over about 148 km². Its green spaces include Parc de la Falaise et de la chute Kabir Kouba, the Marais-du-Nord nature reserve, the Saint-Charles River linear park and the Mont-Bélair nature park.

    • Housing

      Bungalows from the 1950s onward, older houses along the first roads and a denser fabric in Loretteville, with mansard-roofed and Boomtown-style houses.

    • Nearby

      Réserve naturelle des Marais-du-Nord · Mont Bélair · Wendake

    • Good to know

      Public transit is provided by the Réseau de transport de la Capitale. The territory is large: check the route to your workplace before viewing a unit.

    History

    The history of La Haute-Saint-Charles.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    3. 1949

    4. 2002

      Creation of the borough

    5. 2009

      Val-Bélair joins the borough

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1647

      //

      Settlement began in 1647 on the seigneuries of Saint-Ignace, to the east, and Saint-Gabriel, to the west. Hills and waterways dictated the placement of farms, served by roads that would become Rue Racine, Boulevard Bastien, Boulevard de l’Ormière and Avenue du Lac-Saint-Charles.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · Chapter 02

      //

      Around Lac-Saint-Charles and Saint-Émile, the poor soil limited agriculture. The parish of Saint-Ambroise-de-la-Jeune-Lorette became the town of Loretteville, the area’s commercial and institutional centre. Leatherworking, influenced by the proximity of Wendake, and the power of the Kabir Kouba falls gave rise to a small industry.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1850 – 1965

      //

      Around 1850, the railway made the area easier to reach, attracting vacationers. Cottages multiplied, notably at Château-d’Eau, created in 1926 and then attached to Loretteville in 1965. The first drinking water main to Québec City, drawn from the Saint-Charles River, was laid in 1854, and the present water tower was built in 1949.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 2002 – 2009

      //

      The borough was created in 2002, during the municipal mergers. It then brought together Loretteville, Lac-Saint-Charles, Saint-Émile and the north of Neufchâtel. On November 1, 2009, the Val-Bélair sector, previously in the Laurentien borough, was added.
