    Neighbourhood profile

    Montcalm: renting an apartment in Québec City’s leafy Upper Town.

    Montcalm is part of Québec City’s Upper Town, west of Old Québec, and the Grande Allée and Chemin Sainte-Foy cross it from east to west. It is a sought-after residential neighbourhood, quiet and leafy, with a lively community life around Avenue Cartier. Its population, older than the city average (average age 49.3 in 2016), is mostly tenants: 58.7% of residents. The neighbourhood borders the Plains of Abraham and Parc des Braves.

    • Neighbourhood life

      Avenue Cartier and Boulevard René-Lévesque, between De Salaberry and Bourlamaque: bars, restaurants and small shops. The Halles du Petit Quartier, the Plains of Abraham, the Musée national des beaux-arts du Québec and the Cinéma Cartier, opened in 1937.

    • Transportation

      The Grande Allée, Boulevard René-Lévesque and Chemin Sainte-Foy cross the neighbourhood from east to west, served by RTC buses. Old Québec is very close.

    • Housing market

      Row houses, plexes and large older units, often with woodwork and high ceilings, and apartment towers built in the 1960s.

    History

    The history of Montcalm.

    From an agricultural suburb to an Upper Town neighbourhood, in five dates and four chapters.

    1. 1639

      First land granted to Jean Bourdon

    2. 1760

    5. 1937

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1639 – 1845

      //

      Until the early 20th century, Montcalm was a suburb of Québec City, at first agricultural. In 1639, Jean Bourdon, its first settler, received a 50-arpent land grant around present-day Avenue Murray. On April 28, 1760, the Battle of Sainte-Foy took place on the site of present-day Parc des Braves, whose land was acquired in 1845 to build the Monument des Braves.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1833

      //

      After the Conquest, English-speaking notables, including James Murray and Samuel Holland, bought the estates left by the French elite. In the early 19th century, the English-speaking bourgeoisie owned large villas there, far from the sanitation problems of the city. As early as 1833, the Faubourg Guénette and its modest dwellings took shape between Avenues Bourlamaque, Crémazie and Cartier.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1908 – 1913

      //

      In 1908, the area became the Town of Montcalm, which banned factories and set a minimum value for new construction. It was annexed to Québec City in 1913, after a referendum in which one of the issues was financing a sewer system.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1900 – 1960

      //

      Between 1900 and 1940, Québec City tripled its area and doubled its population, and Montcalm urbanized quickly. Commerce first set up on Chemin Sainte-Foy, then on Avenue Cartier starting in the 1920s. The neighbourhood’s main characteristics took shape between 1914 and 1960: triplexes, then apartment and office towers in the 1960s.
