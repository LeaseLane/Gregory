    The rules of the Tribunal administratif du logement, explained.

    Civil Code, procedures, deadlines and fees: every answer follows the same order. The situation, the rule and its source, each party’s options, the next step.

    For owners

    • Notice of change: windows and deadlines
    • 2026 increase: 3.1% base rate and the TAL tool
    • Unpaid rent: recourse from 3 weeks of delay
    • Repossession, eviction and the Bill 65 moratorium

    For tenants

    • Accepting or refusing an increase, within the month
    • Repossession, eviction and protection for those 65 and over
    • Lease assignment and sublet (Bill 31)
    • Repairs, emergencies and access to the unit

    Revised with every change published by the TAL, revision date shown. The year’s rate, fees, official forms and templates: the base is reviewed every January, every November and whenever a law changes.

    Frequently asked questions

    Questions about Cléo.

    These answers come from the questions asked to Cléo. Our team validates and dates them before publication.

    Ask CléoAll questions
    01How does Cléo respond to my tenants and prospective tenants, and when does a human take over?

    Cléo answers 24/7, French first, then in the language of the person it is talking to. It gives general information on the Civil Code and TAL rules that apply to both parties, books viewings and sorts emergencies. It hands over to a member of the team for an emergency, a dispute or formal notice, a repossession, an eviction or a termination, a harassment or discrimination complaint, or whenever asked. It announces every handoff: to whom, why and within what time frame.

    02Can Cléo answer in English or another language?

    Yes. Cléo answers first in French, then in the language of your message, including English. Official documents, such as the lease, remain in French.

    03What is the TAL, the former Régie du logement?

    The Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL) replaced the Régie du logement on August 31, 2020. It decides disputes between tenants and landlords of residential units in Québec, and publishes the rent increase calculation method each year.
