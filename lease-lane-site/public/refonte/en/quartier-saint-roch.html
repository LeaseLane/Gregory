    Neighbourhood profile

    Renting an apartment in Saint-Roch.

    Saint-Roch is in the Lower Town, at the foot of the Québec City hill. Rue Saint-Joseph Est, reopened to the sky, is its shopping street; Boulevard Charest and Rues de la Couronne and Dorchester bring together offices and shops. The Gare du Palais is nearby.

    It has lofts and condos in former industrial buildings, plexes built in the late 19th century and apartment buildings from the 1940s. The Bibliothèque Gabrielle-Roy, the Saint-Roch church and the Jardin de Saint-Roch are local landmarks.

    • Housing

      Condos and lofts in converted former industrial buildings, and units above shops.

    • Nearby

      Bibliothèque Gabrielle-Roy · Église Saint-Roch · Gare du Palais

    • Good to know

      One of the best-served areas for public transit. It is easy to live here without a car.

    History

    The history of Saint-Roch.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    3. 1864

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1620 – 1829

      //

      Saint-Roch is one of the oldest suburbs of Québec City. In the early 17th century, Champlain envisioned a model city there named Ludovica. In 1620, the Récollets built a convent on the banks of the Saint-Charles River, the future first Hôpital général of the city. In 1692, they built a chapel dedicated to Saint Roch, which then gave its name to the street, the suburb, the parish, founded in 1829, and then the neighbourhood.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1845 – 1880

      //

      The neighbourhood took off in the mid-18th century with the shipyards on the Saint-Charles River. In 1857, a market hall opened on Place Jacques-Cartier. In 1864, Guillaume Bresse’s mechanical shoemaking business launched dozens of shoe factories. Despite the fires of 1845, 1866 and 1870, Saint-Roch enjoyed unprecedented prosperity from 1880.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1950 – 1974

      //

      During the first half of the 20th century, Rue Saint-Joseph was Québec City’s great shopping centre, with the Paquet, Pollack, Syndicat de Québec and Laliberté department stores. Two-thirds of the city’s population then lived in Saint-Roch or Saint-Sauveur. From the 1950s, the suburbs drew families away, and the neighbourhood’s population fell from about 20,000 to 5,000. In 1974, part of Rue Saint-Joseph was covered with a roof: this was the Mail Saint-Roch.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 1990 – 2017

      //

      In the late 1990s, the City launched a revitalization program. The mall was demolished from 2000 to 2007, façades were renovated and former factories became offices, artists’ studios or housing co-operatives. IT and video game companies set up around Boulevard Charest. Between 1996 and 2017, the number of people working in the neighbourhood rose by 40%.

    01Where does the name Saint-Roch come from?

    From a chapel that the Récollets dedicated to Saint Roch in 1692, when an epidemic struck the colony. The name then passed to the street, the suburb, the parish and the neighbourhood.

    02What types of housing are there in Saint-Roch?

    Lofts and condos in former industrial buildings, plexes built in the late 19th century and apartment buildings from the 1940s.

    03How has Saint-Roch changed?

    Since the late 1990s, the neighbourhood has been revitalized: demolition of the Mail Saint-Roch from 2000 to 2007, renovation of façades and conversion of former factories. The number of people working there rose by 40% between 1996 and 2017.
