    Town profile

    Renting an apartment in L’Ancienne-Lorette.

    L’Ancienne-Lorette is a town in the Capitale-Nationale region that is part of the Québec City agglomeration. Its 7.63 km² territory is completely enclaved within Québec City. The Lorette River, a tributary of the Saint-Charles River, runs through it from northwest to southeast.

    In the 2021 census, the town had 16,970 residents. Its French-language public schools come under the Centre de services scolaire des Découvreurs, and the town publishes its municipal newspaper, Le Lorettain, four times a year.

    • Housing

      Mostly single-family houses, with duplexes and a few apartment buildings.

    • Nearby

      Boisé de la rivière Lorette · AquaGym Élise-Marcotte · Polyvalente de L’Ancienne-Lorette

    • Good to know

      Public transit is run by the Réseau de transport de la Capitale, as in the rest of the agglomeration. The town is small, about 7.6 km², and the airport is very close.

    History

    The history of L’Ancienne-Lorette.

    The key dates, then the story in four chapters.

    2. 1697

    3. 1967

      The village gains town status

    4. 2002

      Merger with the City of Québec

      The town is reconstituted

    1. 01

      Chapter 01 · 1674

      //

      In 1674, the Jesuit Pierre Chaumonot had a chapel built where the present cemetery stands. It served the Huron-Wendat who had taken refuge near Québec City after the war against the Iroquois. Healed after a pilgrimage to the Italian shrine of Loreto, Father Chaumonot placed the mission under the patronage of Notre-Dame de l’Annonciation, and the place took the name Lorette.

    2. 02

      Chapter 02 · 1697 – 1909

      //

      In 1697, the Huron-Wendat settled farther away, at Jeune-Lorette. Their former village became Vieille-Lorette, or Ancienne-Lorette, and continued to develop with French settlers. Over time, the parish lost part of its territory to Saint-Félix-de-Cap-Rouge in 1872, then to Saint-Gérard-Majella in 1909.

    3. 03

      Chapter 03 · 1947 – 1987

      //

      In 1947, the village separated from the parish under the name Notre-Dame-de-Lorette. It gained town status in 1967 and took back the name Ancienne-Lorette. The parish, which included the airport grounds, was merged with Sainte-Foy on January 1, 1971. In 1987, the town asked that its official name become L’Ancienne-Lorette.

    4. 04

      Chapter 04 · 2002 – 2006

      //

      On January 1, 2002, the town was merged into Québec City. In the June 20, 2004 referendum, 61.7% of voters chose to reconstitute it. L’Ancienne-Lorette became a full-fledged town again on January 1, 2006, within the Québec City agglomeration.

    01Where is L’Ancienne-Lorette?

    In the Québec City agglomeration, in the Capitale-Nationale region. The town is entirely surrounded by Québec City, between the Les Rivières borough to the east and Sainte-Foy–Sillery–Cap-Rouge.

    02Where does the name L’Ancienne-Lorette come from?

    The name recalls the shrine of Loreto, in Italy. When the Huron-Wendat left the mission in 1697 for Jeune-Lorette, the old village became Vieille-Lorette, then L’Ancienne-Lorette.

    03Is Jean-Lesage Airport in L’Ancienne-Lorette?

    The airport is just west of the town, on land that once belonged to the parish of L’Ancienne-Lorette. This territory has been part of Québec City since the parish merged with Sainte-Foy in 1971.
